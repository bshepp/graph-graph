# Emergent geometry and emergent dimension from graphs and local graph dynamics — literature mapped onto the graph-graph sandbox

Compiled 2026-10-16. Scope: how the literature defines and finds emergent geometry/dimension in graph models; which model classes give flat 2D structure versus tree-like/hyperbolic structure; known transitions; what the `sheet` / `grown` / local-rule programme can borrow or test.

Conventions used below. Each paper entry gives authors, year, venue, arXiv/DOI, one or two sentences on what it establishes, then a **Map:** sentence graded *direct* / *analogous* / *speculative*. "[unverified: ...]" marks anything not confirmed from the source itself in this survey (usually: venue or a detail recalled from memory and not found on the fetched page). Project terms: `sheet` (degree cap c=6, closure bias beta, flat 2D disc up to N* ~ exp(3.3 + 5.4 beta), then boundary roughening, fjords, tree-like); `grown` (degree-capped frontier growth, no closure bias, exponential ball growth); d_eff (log|B(v,r)| vs log r slope) with window-stability audit; "dead corner" (capped boundary node whose boundary neighbours are also capped, so closure can never fire).

## Q1. Quantum graphity (Konopka, Markopoulou, Severini, Smolin 2006–2008) and its statistical-mechanics descendants: Hamiltonians, low-temperature phases, is a flat 2D phase found?

### Takeaway
Quantum graphity's Hamiltonian penalises deviation from a target valence and rewards short cycles of a preferred length; its low-temperature "lattice" phase is argued, not derived, and the specific candidate lattices are the 2D square lattice (preferred loop length 4) or the 3D diamond lattice (loop length 6). The descendants that actually simulate (Conrady 2011, Chen–Plotkin 2013) find low-energy states that are *2D triangulations with defects* (conical singularities, handles), a first-order or size-drifting transition, and a super-extensive-entropy argument that the ordered phase cannot survive N -> infinity at fixed couplings — the closest equilibrium analogue of the sandbox's finite N* window.

### Cited Findings
- Konopka, Markopoulou, Smolin (2006), "Quantum Graphity", arXiv:hep-th/0611197. Degrees of freedom live on the complete graph on N vertices; high-temperature phase is permutation-symmetric and disordered, low-temperature phase breaks permutation symmetry to the translation group of a low-dimensional lattice ("geometrogenesis"). Hamiltonian H = H_links + H_vertices + H_loops + H_hop + H_LQG: valence term V Σ_a (v0 − Σ_b J_ab)^2 with v0 = 4 in the worked example; loop term −Σ_L (1/L!) B(L) Π M with B(L) = B0 B^L, so loop lengths are weighted by B^L/L!, peaked at a preferred loop length L*; L* = 4 selects a square-lattice candidate, L* = 6 a diamond-lattice candidate. The paper states the lattice result is argued, not proved ("We argue (but do not prove)"; "We cannot fully answer this question at this point"). — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0611197); [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/hep-th/0611197)
  **Map (analogous):** the valence term is the equilibrium cousin of the sandbox's hard degree cap; the preferred-loop term is the cousin of the triangle-closure bias beta (L* = 3 would be the sandbox's choice; the graphity papers use even L* because the quantum string-net version only allows even loops). The sandbox gets the flat lattice *from growth*, not from an energy minimum.
- Konopka, Markopoulou, Severini (2008), "Quantum Graphity: a model of emergent locality", Phys. Rev. D 77, 104029, arXiv:0801.0861. Reports "evidence for a low-energy phase in which the graph breaks permutation symmetry and appears ordered, low-dimensional and local", argues thermodynamic stability under local perturbations via free energy of the dominant terms, obtains an emergent U(1) gauge theory via string-net condensation, and restates the dynamics graph-theoretically. The abstract does not give a numerical dimension. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0801.0861)
  **Map (analogous):** "ordered, low-dimensional, local" is asserted via free-energy comparison of candidate lattices; no ball-growth dimension measurement is reported, so the sandbox's d_eff + window-stability machinery is stricter than what graphity used.
- Caravelli, Markopoulou (2011), "Properties of quantum graphity at low temperature", arXiv:1008.1340 [unverified venue: recalled as Phys. Rev. D 84, 024002]. Maps the low-temperature model onto an Ising Hamiltonian; identifies the *average valence* of the graph as an order parameter. — [arXiv PDF](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1340)
  **Map (analogous):** average degree as an order parameter is cheap for the sandbox: in a flat triangulated disc the interior degree is pinned at 6 and the mean degree approaches 6 from below as B/N -> 0; in the tree-like regime it stays well below 6. Worth tracking as a one-number diagnostic of the N* crossover.
- Conrady (2011), "Space as a low-temperature regime of graphs", J. Stat. Phys. 142, 898, arXiv:1009.3195, DOI 10.1007/s10955-011-0135-9. Ensemble: labelled graphs with fixed edge number N_e. Hamiltonian is a sum of local terms: vertices H_v = c_v |deg(v) − 6| ("valence 6 corresponds to flatness"); edges H_e = 0 if the edge is in ≤ 2 triangles, else c_e V_e^2; triangles H_t = −c_t each; tetrahedra H_tau = +c_tau each (c_v, c_e, c_t, c_tau = 1, 10, 7, 500). Ideal ground states are triangulated 2-surfaces (every edge in 1 or 2 triangles, vertex links are single cycles). At T = 0.625, N_e = 180: ~8% of vertices are conical singularities, mean valence 6.7, mean Ricci scalar −0.8, largest component a connected non-orientable closed surface; at T = 2.0 ~40% of vertices are conical and 30–40% of edges are boundary edges. Specific-heat peak drifts to lower T with size (T ≈ 1.875, 1.375, 1.125 for N_e = 45, 90, 180) so the author calls it "premature" to decide between a finite-T and zero-T transition; equilibration at low T is doubtful (acceptance < 1e-3 at T = 0.5). A spectral-dimension estimate via return probability "failed" at these sizes. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1009.3195); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1009.3195)
  **Map (direct for the design target, analogous for the mechanism):** this is the published equilibrium model whose energy *explicitly encodes the sandbox's two ingredients* — degree 6 as flatness and triangles rewarded with an edge allowed in at most 2 triangles. Conrady's order parameter m (fraction of edges with exactly 2 triangles) is essentially 1 − (boundary-edge fraction), i.e. the sandbox's B/N in disguise. His low-T states are closed surfaces with ~8% conical defects, whereas the sandbox's `sheet` is a disc with a boundary and defects that nucleate on that boundary.
- Chen, Plotkin (2013), "Statistical mechanics of graph models and their implications for emergent spacetime manifolds", Phys. Rev. D 87, 084011, arXiv:1210.3372. General Hamiltonian favouring near-constant valency and local rotational symmetry, canonical ensemble at fixed vertex/edge ratio (which controls the emergent dimensionality). Low-energy states are nearly triangulations of 2D manifolds with topological handles and self-intersections, non-trivial fractal dimension and some nonlocal links. The transition to the manifold is first order. Graph entropy is super-extensive (attributed to Erdős), so T_c -> 0 as N -> infinity: "infinite manifolds are always disordered"; remedies discussed are a finite universe, diverging couplings, or long-range interactions between vertex defects. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.3372)
  **Map (analogous, important):** this is the equilibrium version of the sandbox's central result. A geometric regime that exists only up to a size set by the coupling (here T_c(N) -> 0; in the sandbox N* ~ exp(3.3 + 5.4 beta)) is what a defect-nucleation picture predicts. Their proposed cure — long-range interaction between defects — has a sandbox analogue: a rule that lets closure act *across* a fjord (see Q8 and new path 4).

### Inferences
- No quantum-graphity paper demonstrates a flat 2D phase by an intrinsic dimension measurement; the 2D lattice is a candidate minimiser selected by hand-tuned couplings. The simulated descendants (Conrady, Chen–Plotkin) get *2D triangulated surfaces* — which is precisely the sandbox's bulk — but closed, defected, and only at finite size.
- Both simulated papers report defect densities (conical singularities 8% at low T in Conrady) that the sandbox would call "non-flat" by its d_eff audit; the sandbox's `sheet` below N* is cleaner (a genuine degree-6 interior) than any equilibrium sample found here.

### Gaps
- The explicit form of H_hop / H_LQG in graphity is not specified in the source (the authors assume they do not alter the ground state).
- Caravelli–Markopoulou venue not confirmed from the source.
- No graphity-lineage paper reports a geodesic-ball dimension or a spectral dimension that succeeded; Conrady's attempt explicitly failed.

## Q2. Trugenberger's combinatorial quantum gravity (2015–2017) and Kelly–Trugenberger–Biancalana (2019) and follow-ups: energy functional, emergent dimension, transition, order parameter

### Takeaway
The energy functional is a discrete Einstein–Hilbert action built from Ollivier (optimal-transport) Ricci curvature of edges, which for "hard-core" random regular graphs is an exact function of the numbers of triangles, squares and pentagons at each edge. Minimisers of random 2D-regular graphs are *hypercubic* (square-based) lattices of dimension D = d/2 up to defects; the order parameter is the normalised squares-per-edge phi_square -> 1; a continuous transition is reported numerically in D = 2, 3 (later work: first-order-looking at higher degree, and in 2D the geometric phase is a *negatively curved* surface). Triangles are suppressed in this programme, so it is the square-lattice mirror image of the sandbox's triangular sheet.

### Cited Findings
- Trugenberger (2015), "Quantum gravity as an information network: self-organization of a 4D universe", arXiv:1501.01408 [unverified: not fetched; recalled as Phys. Rev. D 92, 084014; recalled content: random regular graphs self-organise into a 4D lattice-like structure under a curvature-type energy]. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1501.01408)
- Trugenberger (2017), "Combinatorial Quantum Gravity: Geometry from Random Bits", JHEP 09 (2017) 045, arXiv:1610.05934, DOI 10.1007/JHEP09(2017)045. Geometric space emerges from random bits in a quantum phase transition "driven by the combinatorial Ollivier-Ricci curvature", i.e. a condensation of short cycles in random graphs; in the ordered geometric phase the action reduces to Einstein–Hilbert; the critical point is proposed as the non-perturbative definition of the theory. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05934)
  **Map (analogous):** "condensation of short cycles" is the equilibrium statement of what beta -> large does in `sheet`. The sandbox's triadic-closure rule on a fixed graph is a local dynamical analogue; whether it condenses cycles into a lattice (vs. cliques) is testable (see Q3 on clique collapse).
- Kelly, Trugenberger (2019), "Combinatorial Quantum Gravity: Emergence of Geometric Space from Random Graphs", J. Phys.: Conf. Ser. 1275, 012016, arXiv:1811.12905. Conference review: second-order transition via condensation of cycles on random regular graphs; entropy area law. — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1811.12905); [IOP](https://iopscience.iop.org/article/10.1088/1742-6596/1275/1/012016)
- Kelly, Trugenberger, Biancalana (2019), "Self-Assembly of Geometric Space from Random Graphs", arXiv:1901.09870 [unverified venue: recalled as Class. Quantum Grav. 36, 125012]. Action A_DEH = −Σ_u d_u Σ_{v in N(u)} kappa(uv) (sum of edge Ollivier curvatures weighted by degree). Exact Ollivier curvature for hard-core graphs (no two short cycles at an edge share another edge) in terms of triangle, square and pentagon counts. Ensemble: random d-regular graphs, focus on bipartite 2D-regular graphs. Minimisers are "cubic complexes up to defects", locally subsets of Z^D with D = d/2 (each edge in 2D − 2 squares). Order parameter phi_square = normalised squares per edge -> 1 in the geometric phase; control parameter the renormalised inverse coupling beta-tilde = (hbar g)|V|^(2/D − 1); numerical evidence (diverging correlation length) for a continuous transition in D = 2 and D = 3. Triangles stay at a vanishing fraction; pentagons suppressed; "defects are dynamically suppressed in the macroscopic limit" is argued heuristically. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.09870); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.09870)
  **Map (analogous, with a sharp contrast):** (i) the exact Ollivier curvature from local cycle counts is a *strictly local* quantity the sandbox can compute per edge without any coordinates — permissible under the project's invariants. (ii) Their claim that defects are suppressed macroscopically is the opposite of the sandbox's finding that a single dead corner is never healed and eventually dominates. This is a genuine point of tension worth stating as such, with the caveat that their ensemble is equilibrium and square-based.
- Kelly, Trugenberger, Biancalana (2021), "Emergence of the circle in a statistical model of random cubic graphs", Class. Quantum Grav. (2021). For 3-regular graphs of girth ≥ 4 the action minimisers converge in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense to the circle; finite-size evidence for a second-order transition. — [Heriot-Watt portal](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/emergence-of-the-circle-in-a-statistical-model-of-random-cubic-gr/)
- Kelly (2022), PhD thesis "Emergent geometry and discrete curvature in random graphs", Heriot-Watt: higher-degree cases are less conclusive and the transition "appears to become first-order". — [ROS repository](https://www.ros.hw.ac.uk/handle/10399/4591)
- Kelly, Trugenberger, Biancalana (2022), "Convergence of Combinatorial Gravity", Phys. Rev. D 105, 124002, arXiv:2102.02356. The Ollivier-curvature discrete Einstein–Hilbert action converges to its manifold counterpart on sufficiently dense random geometric graphs and on any Gromov–Hausdorff-convergent graph sequence; also a discrete Klein–Gordon action. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.02356)
  **Map (analogous):** supplies the theorem-level justification that Ollivier curvature on a graph is a legitimate Ricci proxy, which matters if the sandbox's discrete Ricci-flow rule (`ricci`) is to be interpreted geometrically.
- Trugenberger (2022), "Emergent time, cosmological constant and boundary dimension at infinity in combinatorial quantum gravity", arXiv:2112.03778 [unverified: not fetched]. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.03778v1)
- Trugenberger (2023), "Combinatorial quantum gravity and emergent 3D quantum behaviour", Universe 9, 499, arXiv:2311.17526. In the 2D case the geometric phase "describes negative-curvature surfaces with two inversely related scales" (UV Planck length, IR curvature radius); between them the surface is smooth with spectral and Hausdorff dimension 2; beyond the curvature radius the spectral dimension becomes 3 and the surface is read as a Lorentzian de Sitter surface. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17526); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2311.17526)
  **Map (analogous, cautionary):** even in the "geometric" phase of this programme, the 2D result is *not flat*: it is hyperbolic at scales beyond a curvature radius. So "2D with window-stable d_eff = 2 up to a scale, then something else" is also what this very different model produces; the sandbox's N* is a finite-*size* crossover, Trugenberger's is a finite-*scale* crossover, and the sandbox's d_eff audit could in principle tell them apart (see new path 2).
- Trugenberger (2024), "Dark matter and dark energy in combinatorial quantum gravity", Class. Quantum Grav. (2024), arXiv:2409.09385. Dark energy from ground-state curvature at finite coupling; dark matter as metastable structural variants ("allotropes") of the discrete space-time crystal. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.09385v1)
- Trugenberger (2025/26), "Networks as the fundamental constituents of the universe", J. Phys. Complex. 6 (2025) 042001, arXiv:2512.17676 (v2 Apr 2026). Review: a UV fixed point of a statistical model on random networks governed by Ollivier–Ricci curvature separates a geometric phase (space) from a random phase (matter); black-hole holography attributed to the expander property of random regular graphs. A search-result summary of this preprint reports Hausdorff dimension 2.1–2.2 for N ≤ 1000 in the geometric phase, reverting toward 2 at larger N [unverified: that number was not on the abstract page fetched]. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.17676)
- (Order parameter via cycles) "Topological Network Entanglement as Order Parameter for the Emergence of Geometry" (2017), arXiv:1710.00221 [unverified: authors not confirmed]. Derives entanglement-type quantities in terms of relative numbers of 4-cycles and 6-cycles and argues they separate a random phase from a lattice-like geometric phase. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1710.00221)

### Inferences
- The programme's emergent dimension is set by degree through D = d/2 for square lattices; the triangular analogue is D = 2 at degree 6 (triangular lattice {3,6}). The sandbox's cap c = 6 is therefore the triangle-based counterpart of their degree-4 bipartite 2D phase.
- Their order parameter generalises directly: for a triangular sheet, phi_triangle = (triangles per edge)/2 -> 1 in the flat bulk and < 1 on boundaries/fjords. This is a local, coordinate-free order parameter the project does not currently use.

- No paper in this lineage treats a *growth* process; all are equilibrium ensembles (simulated annealing / MCMC on random regular graphs).

## Q3. "The birth of geometry in exponential random graphs" (2021) and related work on random graphs with triangle/square terms (incl. Gorsky/Valba): geometric phase, defects

### Takeaway
The 2021 paper (authors are Akara-pipattana, Chotibut, Evnin — *not* Chapman) shows that rewarding triangles or squares directly makes exponential random graphs collapse, and that a Hamiltonian depending only on length-2 path statistics yields roughly constant degree with very many triangles and squares and statistically frequent small lattice patches and cubes. The surrounding literature on fixed-degree random graphs with triangle terms finds "clustering" and "shattering" transitions into clique clusters — i.e. the generic fate of triangle bias without a degree cap is clique condensation, not a lattice. No Gorsky/Valba paper could be located by search in this survey.

### Cited Findings
- Akara-pipattana, Chotibut, Evnin (2021), "The birth of geometry in exponential random graphs", J. Phys. A: Math. Theor. 54, 425001, arXiv:2102.11477, DOI 10.1088/1751-8121/ac2474. Simple ERG models whose Hamiltonian depends only on properties of paths of length 2 avoid the collapse of naive triangle/square-count models, keep vertex degree roughly constant, and produce triangles and squares "in very large numbers"; small patches of regular lattices and cubes appear statistically often although never put in by hand. Motivated as a graph route to Euclidean quantum gravity. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.11477); [APS March Meeting abstract](https://archive.aps.org/mar/2022/b08/6)
  **Map (analogous):** the collapse of naive triangle-rewarding ERGs is the equilibrium version of the question "what does triadic closure do to a fixed graph?" in the sandbox. The sandbox's degree cap is what prevents clique collapse (K_7 is impossible at c = 6), so the sandbox's `triadic` rule on a capped graph is a controlled version of their uncollapsed regime.
- Akara-pipattana (2020), BSc thesis, Chulalongkorn Univ. (Evnin co-advisor), "Statistical Mechanics of Emergent Geometry in Random Graphs": edges self-assemble into triangles and hexagons in the modified models. — [CUIR PDF](https://cuir.car.chula.ac.th/bitstream/123456789/79065/1/63-SP-PHYS-012-Pawat%20Akarapipattana.pdf)
- "Transitions in loopy random graphs with fixed degrees and arbitrary degree distributions" (2020), arXiv:2008.11002 [unverified: authors not shown in search results; recalled as López & Coolen]. Maximum-entropy ensembles with fixed degrees and a tunable number of triangles show a *clustering* transition (isolated cycles -> cycle clusters) and a *shattering* transition into many small cliques; both locations are size-dependent; the shattering is entropic. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.11002); [IP Paris portal](https://researchportal.ip-paris.fr/en/publications/transitions-in-random-graphs-of-fixed-degrees-with-many-short-cyc/)
  **Map (analogous):** "shattering into cliques" is the failure mode the sandbox's degree cap forbids. If the project ever relaxes the cap, this predicts clique condensation rather than a sheet.
- "Motif enrichment as a driver of scale-free behavior in rewired random regular graphs" (2026), arXiv:2604.23152 [unverified: authors not shown]. Triangle-poor and triangle-rich phases in rewired random regular graphs; the inter-cluster subgraph acquires a power-law degree distribution with exponent near 2. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.23152)
- Kenyon, Radin, Ren, Sadun (2017), "The phases of large networks with edge and triangle constraints", arXiv:1701.04444; DiCarlo, Sadun (2025), "Tripodal structure in undersaturated random graphs", arXiv:2508.16797. Dense-graph (graphon) phase diagrams for fixed edge and triangle densities, with symmetry-breaking phase boundaries and a 2025 "tripodal" region with a discontinuous transition. — [arXiv 1701.04444](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1701.04444); [arXiv 2508.16797](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.16797)
  **Map (speculative):** dense regime; the sandbox is sparse. Relevant only as the rigorous statement that triangle constraints produce multi-block (non-geometric) phases.
- Gorsky / Valba: search for "Gorsky Valba random regular graphs triangles" returned nothing by those authors. [unverified from memory: Avetisov, Hovhannisyan, Gorsky, Nechaev, Tamm, Valba (2016) PRE 94, 062313, "Eigenvalue tunneling and decay of quenched random network" — random regular graphs with a chemical potential for triangles fragment into dense clusters/cliques; Valba, Gorsky (2021) PRD 103, 106013, "Interacting thermofield doubles and critical behavior in random regular graphs".] Not confirmed here.

### Inferences
- Across all equilibrium triangle-bias models located, the geometric outcome requires something that blocks clique formation (hard-core condition in Kelly et al., path-2 Hamiltonian in Akara-pipattana et al., valence terms in graphity). The sandbox's hard degree cap plays exactly that role, which is one reason `sheet` can be flat at all.

### Gaps
- Gorsky/Valba papers not found; the specific "triangle/square terms" results attributed to them in the brief could not be verified in this survey.
- The 2021 J. Phys. A paper's abstract gives no quantitative "geometric phase" or defect classification; the body was not read.

## Q4. Dynamical triangulations (CDT / EDT): phases, Hausdorff and spectral dimension, branched polymers, why 2D random triangulations have d_H = 4 and what restores d_H = 2

### Takeaway
Uniform random planar triangulations (EDT in 2D) have Hausdorff dimension 4: the uniform infinite planar triangulation has ball volume ~ r^4 and ball boundary ~ r^2 (Angel 2003), and the scaling limit is the Brownian map with d_H = 4 (Le Gall). The mechanism is the proliferation of baby universes (regions pinched off by minimal necks of 3 links). Imposing a causal/foliated structure that forbids spatial topology change (CDT) restores d_H = 2 — the 2D uniform infinite causal triangulation is a.s. d_H = 2 and recurrent (Durhuus–Jonsson–Wheater 2010), and it can be grown one triangle at a time (Sisko–Yambartsev–Zohren 2013). Unconstrained higher-dimensional EDT falls into branched-polymer (d_H = 2, gamma = 1/2) or crumpled (d_H -> infinity) phases; CDT's de Sitter phase is the only one with a 4D classical limit. Crucially for the sandbox: *boundary ~ sqrt(volume) holds for the Brownian map as well*, so B ~ sqrt(N) alone does not certify flatness.

### Cited Findings
- Angel (2003), "Growth and percolation on the uniform infinite planar triangulation", Geom. Funct. Anal. 13, 935 [unverified venue; arXiv:math/0208123]. Growth-process construction of the UIPT; "the UIPT has growth rate r^4 up to polylogarithmic factors"; the boundary of the ball of radius r "has growth rate r^2 up to polylogarithmic factors"; site-percolation p_c = 1/2. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0208123)
  **Map (direct, as a null model):** UIPT is the canonical 2D-topology graph that is *not* 2D metrically. Note boundary ~ r^2 while volume ~ r^4, i.e. boundary ~ sqrt(volume): the sandbox's B ~ sqrt(N) signature is shared by this d_H = 4 object. Only the ball-volume exponent (r^2 vs r^4) discriminates.
- Le Gall (2007/2013), Brownian map: uniformly random triangulations of the sphere with n faces, distances rescaled by n^(−1/4), converge to the Brownian map, which is a.s. homeomorphic to the 2-sphere and has Hausdorff dimension 4; proofs go through bijections between planar maps and labelled trees (Brownian snake on the CRT). — [Le Gall, "Random geometry on the sphere", arXiv:1403.7943](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.7943); [Le Gall ICM 2014](https://www.imo.universite-paris-saclay.fr/~jflegall/Article-ICM2014.pdf)
  **Map (direct, as a null model):** the n^(−1/4) rescaling is the statement d_H = 4. The "why" (my reading of the sources, not a quoted explanation): distances in a uniform map are governed by labels on an underlying random tree whose height ~ n^(1/2) and label fluctuations ~ n^(1/4), so geodesic distance scales like n^(1/4), not n^(1/2).
- Ambjørn, Watabiki (1995), "Scaling in quantum gravity", Nucl. Phys. B 445, 129, arXiv:hep-th/9501049. d_H defined from the exponential fall-off of the geodesic two-point function; the entropy exponent gamma is related to baby-universe structure; a quantum-gravity Fisher scaling relation links gamma and d_H; verified in 2D. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9501049)
  **Map (analogous):** gives the sandbox a second, independent estimator of d_H (two-point function decay) to cross-check d_eff.
- Ambjørn, Jain, Thorleifsson (1993), "Baby universes in 2d quantum gravity", Phys. Lett. B 307, 34 [unverified venue], arXiv:hep-th/9303149. Defines the minimum-neck baby universe ("minbu"/"mimbu") as a piece cut off by a loop of three links (minimal area 3); the size distribution of baby universes yields the string susceptibility gamma and characterises surface roughness. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9303149)
  **Map (analogous, strong):** a fjord in `sheet` is a baby universe that *never closes its neck*. The minbu size distribution is a ready-made observable for the fjord population (new path 3).
- "2D Quantum Gravity — Three States of Surfaces" (1996), arXiv:hep-lat/9603013 [unverified authors]. Classifies DT surfaces into flat, crumpled and branched-polymer states; proposes minbu analysis as the probe, with a sharp shift of the baby-universe size distribution at the branched-polymer transition. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9603013)
- Ambjørn, Barkley, Budd, Loll (2011), "Baby Universes Revisited", arXiv:1110.3998. Confirms the minimal-neck result of Jain–Mathur and corrects the formula for non-minimal necks. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1110.3998)
- Ambjørn, Loll (1998), "Non-perturbative Lorentzian quantum gravity, causality and topology change", Nucl. Phys. B 536, 407, arXiv:hep-th/9805108. Exactly solvable 2D model summing only over triangulations with a causal (sliced) structure; its continuum limit differs from Liouville/matrix-model 2D gravity; allowing the spatial slices to change topology ("baby universe creation") recovers the Liouville/matrix-model results. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9805108)
  **Map (analogous, strong):** the single constraint "no baby universes" is what separates a d_H = 2 ensemble from a d_H = 4 one in 2D. The sandbox's dead-corner fjord is a baby-universe nucleation site. A rule variant that forbids fjord formation (or heals necks) is the sandbox analogue of the CDT constraint (new path 4).
- Durhuus, Jonsson, Wheater (2010), "On the spectral dimension of causal triangulations", J. Stat. Phys. 139, 859 [unverified venue], arXiv:0908.3643. Infinite-volume ensemble of causal triangulations is equivalent to the uniform infinite planar tree; Hausdorff dimension a.s. 2; a.s. recurrent so spectral dimension ≤ 2; equals 2 for the reduced ensemble. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3643)
  **Map (direct, cautionary):** a structure that *is* a tree in disguise can have d_H = 2. "Tree-like" is not synonymous with "exponential": critical/generic trees have polynomial growth (d_H = 2). The sandbox's `grown` graphs are exponential, hence *supercritical* tree-like, a different class.
- Durhuus, Jonsson, Wheater (2007), "The spectral dimension of generic trees", J. Stat. Phys. 128, 1237 [unverified venue], arXiv:math-ph/0607020. Generic random trees have spectral dimension 4/3 (mass exponent 1/3); combs interpolate 1 to 3/2. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0607020); [DMTCS version](https://dmtcs.episciences.org/3507)

## Q5. Causal set sequential growth (Rideout–Sorkin) and whether any local sequential growth is known to produce manifold-like sets

### Takeaway
Classical sequential growth (CSG) is the one well-developed theory of *local, element-by-element* growth of discrete spacetime. Its generic outputs are not manifoldlike: transitive percolation gives an early tree-like phase and then de Sitter-like cardinality growth of causal diamonds (Ahmed–Rideout), but interval-abundance tests fail (Glaser–Surya), and the N -> infinity continuum limits are semi-orders with no spatial structure (Brightwell–Georgiou). Sorkin has argued a manifoldlike outcome requires quantal, not classical, growth.

### Cited Findings
- Rideout, Sorkin (2000), "A classical sequential growth dynamics for causal sets", Phys. Rev. D 61, 024002, arXiv:gr-qc/9904062. Derives from Bell causality and discrete general covariance a family of stochastic growth dynamics where each step adds one element; transitive percolation is the special case t_n = t^n with t = p/(1−p) (each new element links to each existing one independently with probability p). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/9904062)
  **Map (analogous):** CSG is the closest formal relative of "add one node with a local attachment rule". The sandbox's `grown` is a graph (not a poset) version of percolation-style attachment; the sandbox's `sheet` adds a closure step that CSG has no analogue of.
- Rideout, Sorkin (2001), "Evidence for a continuum limit in causal set dynamics", Phys. Rev. D 63, 104011, arXiv:gr-qc/0003117. As N -> infinity one can tune p to hold coarse-grained physics fixed at any scale. — [arXiv PDF](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/gr-qc/0003117v2)
- Ahmed, Rideout (2010), "Indications of de Sitter spacetime from classical sequential growth dynamics of causal sets", Phys. Rev. D 81, 083528 [unverified venue], arXiv:0909.4771. Near one of the cyclic "origin elements" the effective dynamics "yields an initial period of de Sitter-like exponential expansion"; cardinalities of large causal diamonds are de Sitter-like functions of discrete proper time under (1 − q) << 1, n >> 1/(1 − q). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.4771v1); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0909.4771)
- Surya (2019), "The causal set approach to quantum gravity", Living Rev. Relativ. 22, 5, arXiv:1903.11544. (Per the search summary of the review) after a post the percolation universe "enters a tree-like phase and then a de Sitter-like phase"; Glaser and Surya (2013) found the abundance of causal intervals is not de Sitter-like, "thus this is not strictly a manifold-like phase"; Brightwell and collaborators showed the N -> infinity limits are semi-orders which "though temporally ordered, have no spatial structure at all". — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1903.11544); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11544)
  **Map (analogous):** "tree-like first, then something else, but never manifoldlike under classical local growth" is the causal-set analogue of `grown` having no dimension.
- Sorkin, talk (2014), Perimeter Institute: proposes modifications of CSG aimed at manifoldlike causal sets and argues that a truly manifold-like causal set would need quantal dynamics. [Secondary: search summary of a talk PDF.] — [PIRSA PDF](https://pdf.pirsa.org/files/14060042.pdf)
- APS SES 2022 abstract, "Spacetime as an evolving causal set": chain-distribution tests of manifoldlikeness; simulations "support the conjecture that the universe goes through an initial stage of being a random tree-like structure". — [APS abstract](https://archive.aps.org/ses/2022/j02/6)
- (2024) Foundations of Physics paper on csg-paths: uncountably many growth paths sharing a common beginning before branching. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10701-024-00767-5)
- Kleitman–Rothschild dominance of the uniform measure by three-layer orders (not manifoldlike) [unverified: from memory; covered in the Surya review but not read here].

### Inferences
- Across CSG the obstacle is entropic: generic local growth favours high-entropy, spatially structureless orders. This is the same super-extensive-entropy obstruction Chen–Plotkin (Q1) identify for graph Hamiltonians, and it is consistent with the sandbox's observation that geometry must be *maintained* by a bias (beta) and is lost once a defect decouples a region from that bias.

### Gaps
- No paper located in which a classical local sequential growth provably yields a manifoldlike causal set; the literature's own statements are negative or conjectural.
- The Surya review's body was not read; CSG specifics come from the search summary of it.

## Q7. Network geometry (Boguñá et al. 2021; hyperbolic random graphs; Network Geometry with Flavor; growing simplicial complexes with constraints): is there a known Euclidean-to-hyperbolic transition in growth?

### Takeaway
The complex-networks literature's growth models with triangle gluing and saturation constraints (Wu–Menichetti–Rahmede–Bianconi 2015; Bianconi–Rahmede NGF 2016/2017) are the closest published relatives of `sheet`, but they cap *links* (each link in at most m triangles), not *degrees*, and the result is uniformly hyperbolic: exponential growth, infinite Hausdorff dimension, finite spectral dimension, planarity for m = 2. NGF is hyperbolic for all flavours and dimensions except the trivial chain. No published growth model with an emergent Euclidean-to-hyperbolic *transition* as a parameter varies was found; the one tunable-dimension growth model located is for trees (vertex splitting, d_H from 1 to infinity).

### Cited Findings
- Boguñá, Bonamassa, De Domenico, Havlin, Krioukov, Serrano (2021), "Network geometry", Nat. Rev. Phys. 3, 114–135, arXiv:2001.03241, DOI 10.1038/s42254-020-00264-4. (Bianconi is *not* an author.) Three notions of network geometry: shortest-path (metric) geometry, hidden/latent-space geometry, and effective geometry induced by dynamical processes; argues they are closely connected. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2001.03241)
  **Map (analogous):** the sandbox's d_eff is shortest-path geometry; its activation/majority/Hebbian dynamics on fixed graphs are the "effective geometry from dynamics" notion. The review is the right framing citation for asking whether dynamical rules "see" the same geometry as geodesic balls.
- Krioukov, Papadopoulos, Kitsak, Vahdat, Boguñá (2010), "Hyperbolic geometry of complex networks", Phys. Rev. E 82, 036106, arXiv:1006.5169. Heterogeneous (scale-free) degree distributions and strong clustering follow from an underlying negatively curved hyperbolic geometry, and conversely metric structure plus heterogeneous degrees implies effective hyperbolicity; edges as non-interacting fermions with energies = hyperbolic distances. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.5169)
  **Map (analogous):** gives the dictionary "degree heterogeneity + clustering <-> hyperbolic". The sandbox's degree cap kills heterogeneity by construction, which by this dictionary removes one driver of hyperbolicity; the tree-like regime of `grown`/`sheet` is hyperbolic *without* heterogeneity, i.e. a different route (branching, not hubs).
- Wu, Menichetti, Rahmede, Bianconi (2015), "Emergent complex network geometry", Sci. Rep. 5, 10073, arXiv:1412.3405. Growth rule: start from one triangle; with probability 1 − p pick a random *unsaturated* link (i, j) and add a new node s with links (i, s), (j, s) (a new triangle on that link); with probability p pick an unsaturated link and add a link between nodes at hopping distance 2, closing every triangle this creates provided no link exceeds the saturation m (each link in at most m triangles). Curvature used: R_i = 1 − k_i/2 + t_i/3 (Knill's definition truncated at 2-simplices), satisfying Gauss–Bonnet chi = Σ_i R_i; bulk nodes R = 1 − k/6, boundary nodes R = (4 − k)/6. — [ar5iv full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1412.3405); [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1412.3405)

Their curvature formula and Gauss–Bonnet check apply verbatim to the sandbox (see Q8).

NGF with flavour s in {−1, 0, 1} grows simplicial complexes of dimension d by non-equilibrium rules; depending on parameters it yields chains, higher-dimensional manifolds, or scale-free small-world networks with communities; reduces to the Bianconi–Barabási model, the stochastic Apollonian network and Complex Quantum Network Manifolds in special cases; obeys a generalised area law; face "generalised degrees" follow Fermi–Dirac, Boltzmann or Bose–Einstein statistics depending on s, d and face dimension. [Attachment rule (1 + s n_face) not confirmed on the abstract page.] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.04539)
- Bianconi, Rahmede (2017), "Emergent hyperbolic network geometry", Sci. Rep. 7, 41974, arXiv:1607.05710.   **Map (direct as a negative control):** NGF with s = −1, d = 2 ("manifold" flavour: each link accepts at most one more triangle) is a planar tree-of-triangles with infinite d_H. ## Q8. Does any paper identify a single local defect that switches a growing planar structure from 2D to tree-like/hyperbolic? Combinatorial curvature (Higuchi, DeVos–Mohar, Keller–Peyerimhoff) and the dead corner

### Takeaway
No paper was found that identifies the sandbox's exact mechanism (a capped boundary node that can never close, seeding a permanent fjord in a degree-capped planar growth). The closest published analogues are: the minimal-neck baby universe in 2D DT (Q4), the singular high-coordination vertex of CDT's bifurcation phase (Q4), and CDT's prohibition of spatial topology change. The combinatorial-curvature literature supplies the right accounting: with kappa(v) = 1 − deg(v)/2 + Σ_f 1/|f|, a triangulated interior at degree 6 is exactly flat, a boundary node of degree k with k − 1 triangles has kappa = (4 − k)/6, so a dead corner (k = 6 on the boundary) carries kappa = −1/3; strictly positive curvature forces finiteness (Higuchi's conjecture, DeVos–Mohar), uniformly negative curvature forces a positive Cheeger constant and exponential growth (Keller–Peyerimhoff), and nonnegative curvature caps growth at polynomial order [unverified: Hua–Jost–Liu].

### Cited Findings

Proves Higuchi's conjecture via a generalised Gauss–Bonnet formula for infinite planar graphs. — cited in [arXiv:1703.04119](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1703.04119) and [Keller–Peyerimhoff](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0903.4793)

So a cap of c = 5 is predicted to close up (icosahedron-like), c = 6 is the unique flat choice, and c = 7 would give uniformly negative curvature (the {3,7} tiling) and exponential growth. - Conrady (2011) (Q1): the defects that spoil 2D surfaces at low temperature are conical singularities (vertices whose link is not a single cycle), ~8% of vertices at the lowest equilibrated temperature. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1009.3195)

### Inferences
- Gauss–Bonnet bookkeeping for a finite triangulated disc with flat interior forces the boundary curvatures to sum to a constant (chi), so negative dead corners must be balanced by positive convex corners elsewhere; the growth-rate consequence comes not from net curvature but from the *geometry of the large faces* (fjords) which make graph distance much longer than "embedded" distance. This reconciles "all interior nodes are degree 6" with exponential growth: the exponential regime is a statement about the fjord network, not about the sheet's bulk. (My inference from the cited curvature results; the sandbox can test it by partitioning nodes into bulk / boundary / fjord-wall classes.)
- The sandbox's N* ~ exp(3.3 + 5.4 beta) has the form of a first-passage time for a Poisson defect-nucleation process with per-site rate ∝ exp(−5.4 beta). The literature analogue is Chen–Plotkin's T_c(N) -> 0 and Conrady's size-drifting specific-heat peak: geometry is metastable against defect nucleation.

### Gaps
- No source found that states the dead-corner mechanism or the "degree-capped planar growth with an un-closable boundary node" scenario.
- Hua–Jost–Liu's quadratic-growth theorem and Higuchi/DeVos–Mohar venues are from memory, not from fetched pages.
- Whether a *sparse* set of negative-curvature boundary defects on an otherwise flat disc produces exponential growth is not covered by any theorem located; Keller–Peyerimhoff require uniform bounds.

## Concrete new paths for the project (testable, each grounded in a specific paper)

### Takeaway
Six measurements/variants follow directly from the sources above; the first three are pure analysis on existing pickles, the last three are rule variants.

### Cited Findings
- Path 1 — Local curvature audit of the sheet. Compute kappa(v) = 1 − deg/2 + Σ_f 1/|f| (equivalently Wu et al.'s R = 1 − k/2 + t/3 when all faces are triangles, with the outer/fjord faces contributing ~0) for every node at every snapshot; check Gauss–Bonnet Σ kappa = chi as a planarity/consistency test; track the total negative curvature mass M_−(N) = Σ_{kappa<0} |kappa(v)| and the count of kappa = −1/3 dead corners. Prediction: M_− is O(1) while the disc is flat and becomes extensive at the N* crossover; the onset of exponential d_eff should coincide with M_−/N reaching a constant. Grounding: [Keller–Peyerimhoff](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0903.4793); [Wu et al. 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1412.3405). Cost: trivial (local counts).
- Path 2 — Separate the three random-geometry classes with two extra estimators. (a) Ball-boundary exponent: |∂B(v,r)| ~ r^1 (flat), ~ r^2 (Brownian-map class), exponential (supercritical tree) — Angel shows UIPT has boundary ~ r^2 with volume ~ r^4, so B ~ sqrt(N) is *not* diagnostic of flatness ([Angel 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0208123)). (b) Spectral dimension from return probability: d_s = 2 for flat sheets and causal triangulations, 4/3 for generic trees, undefined/divergent for exponential graphs ([DJW 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0607020); [DJW 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3643)); Conrady's failure at N_e ≤ 180 ([Conrady](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1009.3195)) warns that this needs the sandbox's larger N. (c) Two-point-function d_H as a cross-check of d_eff ([Ambjørn–Watabiki](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9501049)). Also test the David–Dukes–Jónsson–Stefánsson alternative that the asymptotic exponent drifts continuously with beta rather than crossing over ([0811.3183](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.3183v1)).
- Path 3 — Minbu / baby-universe census of fjords. Define a neck as a short cut (loop or boundary-to-boundary path of length ≤ 3) separating a macroscopic region, count necks and the size distribution of the regions they cut off at each snapshot, and fit the branched-polymer exponent gamma (gamma = 1/2 expected for branched polymers). Prediction: no macroscopic necks below N*, a power-law size distribution above. Grounding: [Ambjørn–Jain–Thorleifsson 1993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9303149); [hep-lat/9603013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-lat/9603013); the Landau-theory statement that branched polymer has d_H = 2, gamma = 1/2 ([2212.11043](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.11043)).
- Path 4 — CDT-style constraint variant: forbid baby-universe formation locally. Add a rule variant in which a new node may bridge two boundary nodes at graph distance 2 across a fjord mouth (Wu et al.'s process (b), [1412.3405](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1412.3405)), or in which a dead corner is allowed one "neck-sealing" closure that violates the cap by one. Prediction (by analogy with Ambjørn–Loll and Durhuus–Jonsson–Wheater: forbidding spatial topology change restores d_H = 2): N* diverges or grows much faster with beta. This also tests Chen–Plotkin's proposed remedy of "long-range interaction between defects" ([1210.3372](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.3372)) in a growth setting.
- Path 5 — Degree-cap scan c in {5, 6, 7} as a Higuchi/DeVos–Mohar test. With triangulated faces, c = 5 gives uniformly positive curvature and must close into a finite object (DeVos–Mohar); c = 6 is flat; c = 7 gives kappa ≤ −1/6 and Keller–Peyerimhoff's bounds predict exponential growth from the start with rate ≥ log(1 + 2q/(q − 1) C). Prediction: the flat window exists only at c = 6 and N*(c = 7) ~ O(1). Grounding: [Keller–Peyerimhoff](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0903.4793) and the DeVos–Mohar result as cited there.
- Path 6 — Order parameter and Ollivier curvature per edge, Kelly–Trugenberger–Biancalana style. Compute phi_triangle (triangles per edge normalised to 2) and the exact Ollivier curvature of each edge from local triangle/square/pentagon counts (formula in [1901.09870](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.09870)), and test (i) whether the edge-curvature distribution develops a negative tail at N*, (ii) whether dead-corner edges are the most negative, and (iii) whether the sandbox's `ricci` rule drives toward or away from phi_triangle = 1. This directly confronts their heuristic claim that defects are suppressed in the macroscopic limit with the sandbox's finding that one un-closable defect dominates. Also compare the sandbox's finite-size crossover with Trugenberger's finite-*scale* crossover (flat below a curvature radius, hyperbolic above, [2311.17526](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17526)) by checking whether d_eff(r) at fixed large N bends at a beta-dependent radius.
