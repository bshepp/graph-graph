Items marked **[secondary only]** were seen only through papers that cite them. ## Key question 1 — Discrete Ricci flow on graphs: does it build geometry or cut bridges?

### Takeaway
Every *flow* in the literature (Ollivier/Lin-Lu-Yau flow of Ni et al., Forman flow of Weber-Saucan-Jost, SDRF of Topping et al.) is a weight dynamics whose fixed point is constant curvature per component and whose practical effect, once surgery is added, is to stretch and cut negatively curved inter-community edges — i.e. it fragments, it does not laminate. The only line that reaches a flat, lattice-like (Z^D) phase from Ollivier curvature is Trugenberger/Kelly's *equilibrium energy minimisation* on degree-regular graphs with a hard-core (triangle-forbidding) constraint, where the flatness motif is the **square**, not the triangle, and the transition is continuous. No deterministic local flow has been shown to do this.

### Cited Findings
- Ollivier, Y. (2009) "Ricci curvature of Markov chains on metric spaces", J. Funct. Anal. 256, 810–864, doi:10.1016/j.jfa.2008.11.001 **[not fetched; from memory]** — defines coarse Ricci curvature of an edge via Wasserstein distance between neighbour measures. Mapping: **direct** — the curvature that `ricci` presumably discretises; a positive edge sits in many short cycles, a negative edge is a bridge.
- Lin, Y., Lu, L., Yau, S.-T. (2011) "Ricci curvature of graphs", Tohoku Math. J. 63, 605–627 **[not fetched; from memory]** — the limit-of-lazy-walk version of Ollivier curvature used by essentially all graph Ricci-flow code. Mapping: **direct** (choice of curvature definition).
- Forman, R. (2003) "Bochner's method for cell complexes and combinatorial Ricci curvature", Discrete Comput. Geom. 29, 323–374 **[not fetched; from memory]** — a purely combinatorial edge curvature (for unweighted graphs essentially 4 − deg(u) − deg(v) plus triangle terms in the augmented version). Mapping: **direct** — cheaper than Ollivier and fully local (only degrees and triangles), so it is the natural candidate for a strictly local `ricci` rule.
- Ni, C.-C., Lin, Y.-Y., Luo, F., Gao, J. (2019) "Community detection on networks with Ricci flow", Sci. Rep. 9, 9984, doi:10.1038/s41598-019-46380-9, arXiv:1907.03993 — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03993); [full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1907.03993). Flow is `w_xy^(i+1) = (1 − κ_xy^(i)) · d^(i)(x,y)` with weight read as *length*, applied synchronously ("we update all edge weights simultaneously"), total weight normalised each step; "this discrete Ricci flow process expands negatively curved edges and shrinks positively curved edges"; surgery removes the top-5% weight (longest) edges every 5 iterations or at the modularity plateau; heuristically "inter-community edge weights converge to ∞, intra-community weights to 0"; curvatures "converged to a fix value"; convergence proven only for a toy two-block family (Theorem 4.1, p = 0). Typical runs 20–50 iterations. Mapping: **direct** — this is the canonical implementation of what `ricci` does; its proven/observed endpoint is fragmentation into constant-curvature blobs, not a lattice. Note sign bookkeeping: with weight-as-length, "negative edges stretched then cut" equals the project's "negative edges shrunk/removed" if the project stores weight-as-strength; check that the two conventions agree before comparing.
- Weber, M., Saucan, E., Jost, J. (2017) "Characterizing complex networks with Forman-Ricci curvature and associated geometric flows", J. Complex Netw. 5(4), 527–550, doi:10.1093/comnet/cnw030, arXiv:1607.08654 — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.08654) — introduces Forman curvature and its flow as *edge-based* network descriptors; proposed uses are denoising, clustering and extrapolating network evolution; the abstract makes no claim of convergence to a geometric structure. Companion: Weber, Jost, Saucan (2016) "Forman-Ricci flow for change detection in large dynamic data sets", Axioms 5(4):26, arXiv:1604.06634 — [arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1604.06634). Mapping: **direct** for the curvature choice; **analogous** for the flow — it is used as a filter/backbone extractor, never as a geometry generator.
- Topping, J., Di Giovanni, F., Chamberlain, B. P., Dong, X., Bronstein, M. M. Bober, Monod, Saucan, Webster (2022) arXiv:2207.08026 report that "the impact … lies in the SDRF algorithm, rather than the BFC" and that classical curvatures perform comparably and much faster. A 2026 position paper, arXiv:2601.07419, argues there is no significant relationship between measured over-squashing reduction and predictive performance ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.07419)). Mapping: **analogous** — SDRF is the one curvature rule in the literature that *adds* edges around negative curvature; it is a candidate anti-fragmentation variant of `ricci`, but nobody reports it producing lattice-like structure.
- Bai, S., Lin, Y., Lu, L., Wang, Z., Yau, S.-T. (2020–) "Ollivier Ricci-flow on weighted graphs", arXiv:2010.01802 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.01802v9) — existence and uniqueness for the continuous-time normalised flow; convergence to constant weights proven only for star graphs. Subsequent results: on trees the normalised Lin-Lu-Yau flow converges to constant curvature (Bai, Liu, Lai; **[secondary only]**); on girth ≥ 6 graphs exponential convergence iff constant curvature is attainable ([arXiv:2601.02673](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.02673)); a 2025 survey states convergence for general graphs is still open ([arXiv:2505.15395](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15395v1)). Mapping: **direct** — there is no theorem that the flow converges on graphs with triangles; any `ricci` behaviour the project sees is empirical and schedule-dependent by default.
- Trugenberger, C. A. (2017) "Combinatorial quantum gravity: geometry from random bits", JHEP 2017:45, arXiv:1610.05934 — [abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05934) — "geometric space emerges from random bits in a quantum phase transition driven by the combinatorial Ollivier-Ricci curvature", corresponding to "the condensation of short cycles in random graphs"; in the ordered phase the action reduces to Einstein-Hilbert. Mapping: **analogous** (equilibrium statistical mechanics, not a dynamical rule).
- Kelly, C., Trugenberger, C. A., Biancalana, F. (2019) "Self-assembly of geometric space from random graphs", Class. Quantum Grav. 36, 125012, arXiv:1901.09870 — [full text](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.09870) — ensemble = d-regular graphs with a *hard-core* condition (short odd cycles suppressed); action = discrete Einstein-Hilbert built from an exact Ollivier-curvature formula in terms of triangles, squares and pentagons on each edge; in the bipartite 2D-regular case `κ = −2(1 − 1/D)[1 − φ_□]_+`, Ricci-flat iff an edge carries ≥ 2(D−1) squares; classical solutions are "cubic complexes up to defects", "locally isomorphic to subsets of Z^D" with D = d/2; "second-order network phase transition"; entropy area law |S| ∼ |V|^{2/D}; numerics for D = 2, 3. Mapping: **analogous, strongest available** — flat Z^2 *does* arise from Ollivier curvature, but (i) by Metropolis sampling at a temperature, (ii) with fixed regular degree, (iii) with triangles forbidden, so the closure motif is the 4-cycle. A triangular-lattice target (the project's `sheet`) would need the odd-cycle analogue, which this paper does not treat.
- Kelly, C., Trugenberger, C. A., Biancalana, F. (2021) "Emergence of the circle in a statistical model of random cubic graphs", Class. Quantum Grav. — [HW portal](https://researchportal.hw.ac.uk/en/publications/emergence-of-the-circle-in-a-statistical-model-of-random-cubic-gr/) — for 3-regular graphs both Hausdorff and spectral dimension approach 1 (a circle), transition continuous. Mapping: **analogous** — degree 3 gives D = 1.5 → 1; dimension is set by the degree constraint, not discovered.
- Kelly, Trugenberger, Biancalana (2022) "Convergence of combinatorial gravity", Phys. Rev. D, arXiv:2102.02356 — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2102.02356) — the discrete action converges to the Einstein-Hilbert action on random geometric graphs dense enough to Gromov-Hausdorff converge to the manifold. Mapping: **speculative** — justifies using Ollivier curvature as a geometry diagnostic on `sheet` outputs.

### Inferences
- The two uses of curvature in the literature are opposite: *flows* amplify curvature contrast (negative gets more negative and is cut); *energy minimisation* drives curvature to a flat target. The project's `ricci` as described (shrink negative, reinforce positive) is a flow of the first kind and should be expected to fragment, consistent with the audit's "no dimension" verdict.
- In Kelly-Trugenberger the dimension of the geometric phase is fixed by degree regularity (D = d/2), not emergent; the "emergence" is of *flatness/order*, not of a dimension value. This is the same caveat as the project's `sheet` where the degree cap sets D.
- Triangle closure and square closure are competing geometries: Z^2 is Ricci-flat via squares, the triangular lattice via triangles. A `geometrize` rule that rewards both will have a frustrated target.

- Could not retrieve the full Weber-Saucan-Jost text to confirm which edges the Forman flow removes in their experiments (abstract only).
- No source found that runs a *deterministic* curvature flow (zero temperature) on a fixed-N graph and reports a lattice-like fixed point; absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.
- Topping et al. primary abstract not fetched; SDRF mechanics taken from arXiv:2207.08026 and arXiv:2311.07073 summaries.

## Key question 2 — Triadic closure dynamics: collapse to cliques, known fixed points

### Takeaway
Pure triadic closure on a fixed node set is monotone and terminates at the transitive closure (every component a clique, diameter 1); every published triadic-closure model that reaches a non-trivial stationary state adds a *competing loss term* — link decay, node turnover or a degree cap — and the best-studied such model (Marsili-Vega-Redondo-Slanina) has a first-order sparse↔dense transition with hysteresis. Triadic closure as a *growth* mechanism (new nodes) produces communities and fat tails, and strong clustering is itself a signature of latent geometry (Krioukov), but of a 1D geometry in the proved case.

### Cited Findings
- Davidsen, J., Ebel, H., Bornholdt, S. (2002) "Emergence of a small world from local interactions: modeling acquaintance networks", Phys. Rev. Lett. 88, 128701, arXiv:cond-mat/0108302 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0108302) — fixed N; a random node introduces two of its neighbours (closing a triangle); with probability p a random node is removed with all links and replaced. Yields short path length, high clustering, and scale-free or exponential degree distributions depending on p. Follow-up: Ebel, Davidsen, Bornholdt (2002) "Dynamics of social networks", Complexity 8(2), arXiv:cond-mat/0301260 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0301260). Mapping: **direct** — same `triadic` move on a fixed node set; the stationary state exists only because of the node-turnover term.
- Jin, E. M., Girvan, M., Newman, M. E. J. (2001) "The structure of growing social networks", Phys. Rev. E 64, 046132 **[PRE ref from memory]**; SFI Working Paper 01-06-032 — [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/wop/safiwp/01-06-032.html) — meetings more likely between pairs with mutual friends, unused ties decay, and each node has a cap on friendships; simulations reproduce high transitivity and strong community structure. Mapping: **direct** — degree cap + decay is the regulariser that keeps triadic closure from saturating; the project's `sheet` already uses a degree cap.
- Marsili, M., Vega-Redondo, F., Slanina, F. (2004) "The rise and fall of a networked society: a formal model", PNAS 101(6), 1439–1442, arXiv:cond-mat/0307321 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0307321) — local search (links formed through neighbours of neighbours) plus random link decay; "first-order transition between a sparse network and a densely connected one, with hysteresis for intermediate parameter values"; mean-field theory reproduces degree distribution and clustering. Mapping: **direct** — gives the phase diagram the project's open "does `triadic` collapse extent" question should be read against: with decay λ the answer is bistable (sparse vs dense), with λ = 0 it is dense.
- Bianconi, G., Darst, R. K., Iacovacci, J., Fortunato, S. (2014) "Triadic closure as a basic generating mechanism of communities in complex networks", Phys. Rev. E 90, 042806, arXiv:1407.1664 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.1664) — *growth* models (new node attaches, then closes triangles) produce communities, fat-tailed degree and high clustering; communities "more pronounced in sparser graphs and disappear at high link density or high randomness"; with fitness there is a phase transition to overlapping groups. Mapping: **analogous** — supports the project's growth-vs-fixed-N split: closure during growth organises mesoscale structure, closure at high density erases it.
- Krioukov, D. (2016) "Clustering implies geometry in networks", Phys. Rev. Lett. 116, 208302, arXiv:1604.01575 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01575) — random graphs with fixed expected degree and clustering per node are equivalent to random geometric graphs on the real line if clustering is sufficiently strong. Mapping: **analogous** — a theorem that triangles imply *some* latent metric; the proved case is 1D, so clustering alone does not certify a 2D sheet.
- Gross & Blasius 2008 (full citation in Q3) list "self-organized topology from simple local rules" as a recurring theme of adaptive networks — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0709.1858). Mapping: context.

### Inferences
- Fixed point of `triadic` alone: closing all open wedges is monotone in the edge set and bounded by the complete graph on each component, so it terminates at the clique partition of the initial components — extent (diameter) collapses to 1 with certainty, only the *rate* is an empirical question. (Elementary; no citation needed, but flagged as my inference.)
- The project's "collapse of extent" would therefore be the λ = 0 limit of Marsili et al.; to see a non-trivial phase one must add `prune` with a rate and scan it, expecting hysteresis.
- Bianconi et al.'s "communities disappear at high density" and Krioukov's "clustering ⇒ 1D geometry" together suggest that triadic closure on a fixed node set drives toward *lower* effective dimension (chains of cliques), never a 2D sheet — consistent with the audit.

### Gaps
- I did not find a paper that explicitly measures Hausdorff/effective dimension of a fixed-N triadic-closure model over time; the "collapse" statement rests on the monotonicity argument plus Marsili et al.'s density transition.
- Jin-Girvan-Newman full text not read; whether the population is fixed is inferred from the abstract (cap on friendships, decay) and secondary descriptions.

## Key question 3 — Adaptive / co-evolving networks and Hebbian plasticity: bistability, hysteresis, schedule dependence

### Takeaway
Adaptive-network models robustly show (a) self-organisation toward a critical connectivity when the rewiring rule couples to a *local* readout of activity (Bornholdt-Rohlf; Bornholdt-Röhl Hebbian), (b) fragmentation/absorbing transitions when rewiring is homophilic (adaptive voter), and (c) hysteresis/first-order transitions when link formation competes with decay (Marsili). On update schedule: for symmetric threshold/majority networks the Goles-Olivos theorem says synchronous update gives only period-1 or period-2 attractors while sequential update gives fixed points; for random Boolean networks Gershenson finds the critical point barely moves with the scheme but cyclic attractors change; Fatès shows asynchrony can *induce* a continuous phase transition (DP or parity-conserving class) in some elementary CA.

### Cited Findings
- Gross, T., Blasius, B. (2008) "Adaptive coevolutionary networks: a review", J. R. Soc. Interface 5(20), 259–271, doi:10.1098/rsif.2007.1229, arXiv:0709.1858 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0709.1858); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2405905) — identifies recurring themes across fields: robust self-organisation toward criticality, spontaneous emergence of heterogeneity/hierarchy, complex dynamics and self-organised topology from simple local rules. Mapping: **direct** (framework for activation+reinforcement+prune as an adaptive network).
- Bornholdt, S., Rohlf, T. (2000) "Topological evolution of dynamical networks: global criticality from local dynamics", Phys. Rev. Lett. 84, 6114, arXiv:cond-mat/0003215 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0003215) — asymmetric threshold network; "quiet nodes grow links and active nodes lose them"; average connectivity converges to K_c = 2 as N → ∞; topological transition at K = 2 where the frozen fraction drops from 1 to 0. Mapping: **analogous, strong** — a local rule that uses a node's own activity history to add/remove links self-tunes to criticality instead of collapsing or saturating; the project's collapse-or-full-activation bistability is what you get *without* such a negative feedback.
- Bornholdt, S., Röhl, T. (2003) "Self-organized critical neural networks", Phys. Rev. Mapping: **direct** — the project's `reinforcement` is the Hebbian half; this paper says the *anti-Hebbian* half (weaken uncorrelated pairs) is what turns bistability into self-tuned criticality. Later: Landmann, Baumgarten, Bornholdt (2020) arXiv:2009.11781, activity-based rewiring reaches a critical state with power-law avalanches — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.11781v1); review Rybarsch & Bornholdt arXiv:1212.3106 — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1212.3106).
- Holme, P., Newman, M. E. J. (2006) "Nonequilibrium phase transition in the coevolution of networks and opinions", Phys. Rev. Mapping: **analogous** (state-topology coupling with a sharp transition).
- Vazquez, F., Eguíluz, V. M., San Miguel, M. (2008) "Generic absorbing transition in coevolution dynamics", Phys. Rev. Böhme & Gross (2011) arXiv:1012.1213: mean-field and pair approximations overestimate the fragmentation threshold by 150–200% — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.1213). Mapping: **analogous** — homophilic `rewire` + `majority` should fragment above a rewiring rate; and mean-field predictions for where will be badly off.
- Marsili, Vega-Redondo, Slanina 2004 (Q2) — first-order transition with hysteresis from local link formation + decay. Mapping: **direct** evidence that hysteresis is a generic outcome of local formation/decay competition.
- Goles, E., Olivos, J. (1981) "Comportement périodique des fonctions à seuil binaires et applications", Discrete Appl. Math. 3, 93–105 **[not fetched; from memory]**; result as restated by Goles, Montealegre, Perrot, Theyssier, "PSPACE-completeness of majority automata networks", arXiv:1501.03992 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.03992) and by arXiv:1606.05496 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1606.05496): for finite threshold (incl. majority) networks with symmetric weights, synchronous update has only fixed points or period-2 cycles (Lyapunov/energy proof); under sequential update with non-negative diagonal the attractors are fixed points only. Infinite networks can have any period unless growth is subexponential (Moran, **[secondary only]**). - Gershenson, C. Mapping: **direct** — a prior for the project's open sync/async question: expect the *location* of a transition to be robust and the *attractor type* (fixed vs oscillating) to change.
- Fatès, N. (2014) "A guided tour of asynchronous cellular automata", J. Cell. Autom. 9(5–6), 387–416, arXiv:1406.0792 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1406.0792) — survey. Fatès, N. Mapping: **direct** method — treat synchrony rate α ∈ (0,1] as a control parameter for `geometrize`/`ricci` rather than a binary choice.

### Inferences
- The project's observation that the barrier exponent depends on schedule is consistent with Fatès/Gershenson: schedule shifts attractor structure and can move a system between absorbing and active phases, but should not be read as a change in the *existence* of the bistability.
- Bornholdt-Rohlf/Bornholdt-Röhl predict that adding one anti-Hebbian term to `reinforcement` converts collapse-or-saturate bistability into a self-tuned critical connectivity; this is the most concrete "borrow".

### Gaps
- Holme-Newman critical value and parameter name not in abstract (not fetched beyond abstract).
- Goles-Olivos 1981 original not accessed; theorem statement taken from two secondary sources that agree.
- No paper found that studies sync vs async specifically for a *curvature* flow on graphs; Ni et al. simply use synchronous updates.

## Key question 4 — Majority dynamics and bootstrap percolation: thresholds, critical droplets, tie-break asymmetry

### Takeaway
Bootstrap percolation (the monotone core of `activation`) has two very different barrier laws: on Z^d the critical system size is exponential in 1/p (L_c ≈ exp(λ/p), λ = π²/18 for r = 2 in 2D — a metastability/critical-droplet effect), while on G(n,p) the barrier is a power-law critical initial-set size a_c ∝ (n p^r)^{−1/(r−1)}. Because bootstrap percolation is monotone, its final set is schedule-independent; any schedule dependence in the project's barrier must come from the non-monotone `reinforcement`/`prune` parts. For majority rules, tie-breaking is not a detail: Gärtner-Zehmakan show a biased tie rule can make the favoured colour win from arbitrarily low density on the 2D torus.

### Cited Findings
- Chalupa, J., Leath, P. L., Reich, G. R. (1979) "Bootstrap percolation on a Bethe lattice", J. Phys. C 12, L31 **[not fetched; from memory]** — origin of the model. Mapping: **direct** (`activation` with threshold r is r-neighbour bootstrap percolation plus de-activation).
- Aizenman, M., Lebowitz, J. L. (1988) "Metastability effects in bootstrap percolation", J. Phys. A 21, 3801 **[not fetched; from memory]** — on Z^2 with r = 2 the critical length scales as L_c ∼ exp(c/p); conjectured the sharp constant. Holroyd, A. E. (2003) "Sharp metastability threshold for two-dimensional bootstrap percolation", Probab. Theory Relat. Fields 125, 195–224, arXiv:math/0206132 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206132) — "I(L,p) → 1 if lim inf p log L > λ, and → 0 if lim sup p log L < λ, where λ = π²/18"; modified model λ' = π²/6; settles the Aizenman-Lebowitz conjecture; earlier numerical estimates (0.245 ± 0.015) were far off. Mapping: **direct, strong analogue** of the "bootstrapping barrier": full activation on a lattice requires a critical droplet whose size is exponential in 1/p; the equivalence L_c ≈ exp(λ/p) is my restatement of p log L → λ.
- Balogh, J., Bollobás, B., Duminil-Copin, H., Morris, R. (2012) "The sharp threshold for bootstrap percolation in all dimensions", Trans. AMS 364, 2667–2701, arXiv:1010.3326 — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1010.3326) — for d > r ≥ 2, p_c([n]^d, r) = (λ(d,r) + o(1)) / log_{(r−1)}(n))^{d−r+1}; Cerf-Manzo had the threshold up to constants. Balogh, Bollobás, Morris (2009) "Bootstrap percolation in high dimensions", arXiv:0907.3097 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/0907.3097v1). Mapping: **direct** — the lattice barrier is a (d−r+1)-fold iterated-log law in n, i.e. an essentially flat threshold at any accessible size.
- Janson, S., Łuczak, T., Turova, T., Vallier, T. (2012) "Bootstrap percolation on the random graph G_{n,p}", Ann. Appl. Probab. 22(5), 1989–2047, arXiv:1012.3535 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.3535); [Project Euclid](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/annals-of-applied-probability/volume-22/issue-5/Bootstrap-percolation-on-the-random-graph-G_np/10.1214/11-AAP822.full) — sharp transition: final active set is n − o(n) or o(n) w.h.p.; critical initial size a_c = (1 − 1/r) t_c with t_c = ((r−1)!/(n p^r))^{1/(r−1)} (as restated by Angel & Kolesnik, [arXiv:1705.06815](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1705.06815)); CLT and stopping-time asymptotics. Mapping: **direct** — the random/small-world topologies in the project should show a *power-law* barrier in n and mean degree, unlike the lattice.
- Gärtner, B., Zehmakan, A. N. (2018) "(Biased) majority rule cellular automata", arXiv:1711.10920 (LATIN 2018 **[venue from memory]**) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10920) — synchronous majority on the 2D torus; unbiased model keeps current colour on a tie, biased model always picks blue on a tie; both show two thresholds p1 < p2 and three regimes (red monochromatic / stable coexistence / blue monochromatic) reached in O(n²) steps; the earlier search snippet of the same paper states that under the biased tie rule "the process ends up in a blue monochromatic configuration with high probability even for initial concentration very close to 0" (snippet, not re-verified against the full text). Mapping: **direct** — the project's `majority` argmax tie-break favouring state 0 is the "biased majority model"; the known consequence is that state 0 can win from very low density on lattice-like topologies, so vote results are not Z2-symmetric by construction.
- Gershenson 2004 and Goles-Olivos (Q3) — synchronous majority may oscillate with period 2; asynchronous converges. Mapping: **direct**.
- A "backtracking dynamical cavity method" paper, arXiv:2303.16536, describes a majority variant where on a balanced neighbourhood "the spin always changes to the opposite value from the previous time step" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16536) — a third tie convention (anti-persistent). Mapping: **analogous**; shows the space of tie rules is {keep, biased, flip}.

### Inferences
- The project's barrier is a bistability in an *adaptive* system; the closest rigorous analogue is the bootstrap critical droplet. The exponential-in-1/p droplet size is lattice-specific (needs a d-dimensional nucleation geometry); on small-world/random graphs the analogue is a power law. Measuring which law the barrier follows on `lattice` vs `random` topologies is a cheap way to test whether the project's barrier is "bootstrap-like" at all.
- Because the bootstrap closure is monotone, the synchronous and asynchronous final sets coincide; the project's reported schedule dependence of the barrier exponent must be attributable to `reinforcement` (weights) and pruning, not to `activation` itself. (Inference from monotonicity; standard but uncited here.)
- The `sheet` size law exp(3.3 + 5.4β) is also exponential in a control parameter; a droplet-nucleation interpretation (a boundary must reach a critical perimeter before dead corners dominate) is **speculative** but has the right functional form.

- Aizenman-Lebowitz 1988 not retrieved; the exp(c/p) statement is inferred from Holroyd's p log L → λ.
- Gärtner-Zehmakan thresholds p1, p2 not numerically given in the abstract; the "wins from near-zero density" claim comes from a search snippet of the paper.

## Key question 5 — Graph rewriting systems producing geometry; is growth necessary?

### Takeaway
Nobody has proved that a local rewriting system generates a finite-dimensional manifold-like graph from a seed, and nobody has proved that growth is necessary. The evidence pattern is: (i) fixed-N *equilibrium* models (quantum graphity, Chen-Plotkin, Kelly-Trugenberger) can reach low-dimensional lattice-like minima, but only with a global thermodynamic setting plus hard constraints (regular degree, hard core) and often with defects (handles, nonlocal links) and first-order transitions that depend on microscopic details; (ii) pure local Pachner-move dynamics on triangulations (Euclidean DT) produce *no* extended-geometry phase in D ≥ 3 — only crumpled and branched-polymer phases — until a causal (foliation) constraint is added (CDT); (iii) *growth* models (Bianconi's simplicial-complex growth) do give finite spectral dimension and planar geometries, but hyperbolic/scale-free by default; (iv) graph-rewriting automata and the Wolfram model generate rich structure but their geometry/dimension claims are either not about lattices (self-replication, computation) or assume dimension as a hypothesis.

### Inferences
- There is no theorem of necessity, but every fixed-N success (graphity, Chen-Plotkin, Kelly-Trugenberger) is an *equilibrium ensemble* at a temperature with hard global constraints, and every *dynamical* local-move system without such constraints (EDT, triadic closure, curvature flows) fails to produce extended finite-dimensional geometry. The project's "fixed-graph rules don't create dimension; `sheet` growth does" is therefore in line with, not in tension with, the literature.

## Key question 6 — Geometric "annealing": local moves that preserve manifoldness; checks for latent geometry

### Takeaway
The manifold-preserving local moves are well known (Pachner/bistellar flips on triangulations; Lawson edge flips for planar triangulations) and are exactly the move set used by dynamical triangulations — whose phase structure (Q5) shows that preserving manifoldness locally is not sufficient to reach extended geometry. For *detecting* latent geometry, two tools stand out: Krioukov's clustering-implies-geometry theorem and García-Pérez–Boguñá–Serrano geometric renormalization, under which geometric networks show self-similar scaling across layers.

### Cited Findings
- Pachner, U. (1991) "P.L. homeomorphic manifolds are equivalent by elementary shellings", Eur. J. Combin. 12, 129–145 **[not fetched; from memory]** — bistellar (Pachner) moves connect any two PL-homeomorphic triangulations. Lawson, C. L. (1972) "Transforming triangulations", Discrete Math. 3, 365–372 **[not fetched; from memory]** — edge flips connect all triangulations of a planar point set. Mapping: **direct** candidate move set for a planarity-preserving `geometrize`: if the graph is kept a triangulation, every flip preserves 2-manifoldness by construction; DT literature (arXiv:1409.8144) confirms Pachner moves "split or merge simplices and are ergodic" — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1409.8144).
- Chen & Plotkin 2013 (Q5) — simulated annealing of a valency/rotational-symmetry Hamiltonian reaches near-2D triangulations with handles and nonlocal links — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.3372). Mapping: **direct** precedent for annealing `geometrize`.
- García-Pérez, G., Boguñá, M., Serrano, M. Á. (2018) "Multiscale unfolding of real networks by geometric renormalization", Nat. Phys. 14, 583–589, doi:10.1038/s41567-018-0072-5, arXiv:1706.00394 — [alphaXiv](https://alphaxiv.org/abs/1706.00394); [UB repository](https://diposit.ub.edu/dspace/handle/2445/144078) — embeds networks in a hidden metric space and coarse-grains along it; "real scale-free networks show geometric scaling under this renormalization group transformation", yielding self-similar multilayer unfoldings; motivated by the fact that small-worldness entangles scales. Mapping: **analogous** — a scale-invariance test for geometric structure that is independent of the project's ball-growth `d_eff`; requires an embedding step (hyperbolic), so it is a check, not a rule.
- Krioukov 2016 (Q2) — strong clustering ⇔ random geometric graph on R. Mapping: **analogous** diagnostic.
- Kelly, Trugenberger, Biancalana 2022 (Q1) — discrete Einstein-Hilbert action converges on graph sequences that Gromov-Hausdorff converge to a manifold. Mapping: **speculative** diagnostic: total Ollivier curvature per vertex should approach zero on a flat `sheet`.

### Inferences
- A `geometrize` built from Pachner/edge flips would preserve planarity but, by the EDT result, is expected to wander between crumpled and branched-polymer-like configurations unless an energy (e.g. degree-6 regularity, curvature flatness) biases it; the flip set is the right move set, the energy is the missing ingredient.
- Geometric renormalization is most useful as a *control*: `grown` (exponential, no dimension) should fail to show geometric scaling, `sheet` should pass.

### Gaps
- No paper found that applies geometric renormalization to lattice-like (non-scale-free) networks; its behaviour on a flat sheet is untested in the sources I saw.
- Pachner 1991 and Lawson 1972 details are from memory.

## New paths for the project (each grounded in a specific paper, with the prediction it tests)

1. **Square-closure / hard-core `geometrize` (Kelly-Trugenberger-Biancalana 2019).** Variant: forbid triangles (hard core), fix degree 4, and let the local energy be `κ_e = −2(1 − 1/D)[1 − φ_□]_+` with D = 2, rewarding edges that sit in ≥ 2 squares; run (a) as a Metropolis move set at temperature T and (b) as a deterministic zero-T local flow. Prediction from the paper: (a) shows a continuous transition to a Z^2-like phase with `d_eff → 2` as T is lowered; the project's prior and the DT literature predict (b) stalls in defect-rich glassy states. A clean (a)-vs-(b) gap would pin "no dimension from fixed-N rules" on the absence of a thermal/global ingredient rather than on locality per se.

2. **`ricci` as Ni et al. flow with weight-as-length, normalisation and surgery (Ni, Lin, Luo, Gao 2019).** Implement exactly `w ← (1 − κ)d`, renormalise total weight, cut top-5% every 5 steps, and track modularity, number of components and per-component `d_eff`. Prediction: modularity rises to a plateau and the graph fragments into constant-curvature blobs; `d_eff` of the pieces does not increase. Also run SDRF-style (Topping et al. 2022) *support* moves (add an edge around the most negative edge) as the anti-fragmentation control; prediction from Bober et al. 2022: the choice of curvature (Forman vs Ollivier) matters little, the move set matters.

3. **`triadic` + link-decay scan (Marsili, Vega-Redondo, Slanina 2004; Jin, Girvan, Newman 2001).** Add a decay rate λ (`prune` at rate λ) to `triadic` and sweep λ up and down. Prediction: at λ = 0 extent collapses to cliques (diameter → 1); at intermediate λ a first-order sparse↔dense transition with hysteresis in mean degree; a degree cap (Jin et al.) replaces the dense phase with a stationary high-clustering state. This directly closes the open question "does `triadic` collapse extent" and gives it a phase diagram.

4. **Schedule and tie-break controls for `majority` (Goles-Olivos 1981 via arXiv:1501.03992; Gärtner-Zehmakan 2018; Fatès 2008).** (i) Synchronous vs sequential update on `lattice`: prediction — synchronous runs end in a measurable fraction of period-2 oscillators, sequential runs in fixed points only. (ii) Three tie rules {keep, biased-to-0 (current), flip}: prediction — biased-to-0 yields state-0 consensus from initial state-1 densities well below the keep-rule threshold, i.e. the known Z2 breaking is quantifiable as a shift of (p1, p2). (iii) Scan synchrony rate α ∈ (0,1] for `geometrize`/`ricci`: prediction — if a continuous transition appears, it belongs to directed percolation (measure the density decay exponent).

5. **Barrier-law discrimination for `activation` (Holroyd 2003; Janson-Łuczak-Turova-Vallier 2012).** Run activation *without* reinforcement on `lattice` and on `random`, measuring the critical initial active fraction vs size. Prediction: lattice follows p_c ≈ π²/(18 log L) (r = 2) — a droplet barrier exponential in 1/p; random follows a_c = (1 − 1/r)((r−1)!/(n p^r))^{1/(r−1)} — a power law. Then switch reinforcement on and re-measure; because bootstrap closure is monotone and schedule-independent, any schedule dependence of the barrier exponent must appear only in the second run, isolating `reinforcement` as its source.

6. **Anti-Hebbian term in `reinforcement` (Bornholdt-Röhl 2003; Bornholdt-Rohlf 2000).** Add "weaken an edge whose ends are uncorrelated over a window" and/or "a node that has been active for a window drops an edge, a quiet node adds one". Prediction: the collapse-or-full-activation bistability is replaced by self-organisation to a critical mean degree (K → K_c independent of initial K) with avalanche statistics; measure whether `d_eff` at that critical point differs from the two absorbing states (the literature does not say — this is the new measurement).

Optional diagnostic (García-Pérez-Boguñá-Serrano 2018; Bianconi-Rahmede 2017): compute spectral dimension from the Laplacian spectrum alongside the ball-growth `d_eff` on `sheet` and `grown`; prediction — `sheet` has d_S ≈ d_H ≈ 2 and shows geometric scaling under coarse-graining, `grown` has a vanishing spectral-dimension signature (λ_2 bounded away from 0) and fails the scaling test.
