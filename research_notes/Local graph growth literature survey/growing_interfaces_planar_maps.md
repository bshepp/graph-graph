# Locally-grown planar clusters, triangulations and interfaces: boundary scaling, kinetic roughening, fjords, defect-pinned growth

Scope: literature map for the `sheet` generator (degree cap c=6, boundary attachment + triangle closure with bias beta; compact disc with B ~ sqrt(N) up to N* ~ exp(3.3 + 5.4 beta); then a nucleation-like jump of the boundary exponent above 1, fjords to 0.75 R, bumps to 1.22 R, feature size ~370 nodes; boundary roughness spectrum slope -3.4..-4.7 compact, -2.0..-3.1 rough, 6-fold anisotropy mode at k = 6-8; trigger = "dead corner" = capped boundary node with both boundary neighbours capped; first dead-corner size exponentially distributed across seeds, Weibull shape ~1).

Conventions. "Mapping: direct" = the cited result applies to the sheet as stated; "analogous" = same mechanism in a different model, exponents may transfer; "speculative" = suggestive only. "[unverified]" marks bibliographic or numerical details I could not confirm from the source itself in this session (arXiv abstract pages and ar5iv HTML were read; several PDFs could not be text-extracted locally because no PDF library is installed in the environment).

## KQ1. Eden / Richardson / first-passage percolation: compact shape, KPZ roughness, and what power-spectrum slope to expect

### Takeaway
Radial Eden growth is compact with a deterministic (lattice-anisotropic) limit shape and a boundary whose fluctuations are 1+1 KPZ: alpha = 1/2, beta_growth = 1/3, z = 3/2. For a 1+1 interface the Family-Vicsek structure factor is S(k) ~ k^-(2 alpha + 1), so a KPZ- or EW-rough closed front gives S(k) ~ k^-2, while a Mullins-Herring / super-rough front (alpha = 3/2) gives k^-4. The sheet's rough-phase slope -2.0..-3.1 brackets KPZ (k^-2) and quenched-EW pinned-front values (alpha_loc ~ 0.9, alpha_glob ~ 1.15 -> k^-2.8..-3.3); its compact-phase slope -3.4..-4.7 corresponds to alpha_s = 1.2..1.85, i.e. a super-rough / shape-dominated spectrum, not a kinetically rough one.

### Inferences
- Numbers for the project. Using S(k) ~ k^-(2 alpha_s + 1): rough-phase slope -2.0 -> alpha_s = 0.5 (KPZ/EW); -3.1 -> alpha_s = 1.05 (QEW global exponent ~1.15 would give -3.3). So the rough phase sits between "free KPZ front" and "pinned quenched-disorder front" — consistent with a front that is KPZ-like away from the dead corner and pinned near it. Compact-phase slopes -3.4..-4.7 -> alpha_s = 1.2..1.85, i.e. super-rough. For a closed curve a spectral slope steeper than -3 means the angular spectrum is dominated by the smooth large-scale shape (the deterministic limit shape and its 6-fold harmonics), not by kinetic roughness; in that regime the global exponent read from S(k) does not equal the local one (anomalous scaling, López et al.), so a local-width measurement w(l) over arcs of length l is needed before saying anything about the compact phase's true roughness.
- A spectrum of slope -2 cannot distinguish KPZ from EW (both alpha = 1/2); the discriminator is beta_growth (1/3 vs 1/4) or z, i.e. the time dependence of the width about a *fixed* origin. Takeuchi's centre-of-mass pitfall applies to the sheet, which has no coordinates: the analogue is measuring radial excursion about the seed node in hop distance, never about a centroid-like reference.
- The project's 6-fold mode at k = 6-8 is exactly the lattice-anisotropy limit shape of on-lattice Eden growth on a triangular lattice (a degree-6 triangulated disc is locally the triangular lattice); harmonics at k = 12, 18 should be present and should be excluded from slope fits, as the Eden literature excludes anisotropy before fitting widths.
- Richardson/FPP shape theorems guarantee a compact deterministic limit shape for any Eden-like process with i.i.d. local waiting times. The sheet's compact phase is therefore the expected default; the interesting object is the breakdown at N*, which has no analogue in plain Eden/FPP because there every boundary site stays growable forever.

## KQ2. Peeling of random planar maps (UIPT/UIPQ): B ~ n^{2/3} vs B ~ sqrt(N); is sqrt(N) -> steeper readable as Euclidean -> random-map scaling?

### Takeaway
The "n^{2/3}" exponent is with respect to the number of *peeling steps*, not volume: after n steps the perimeter is ~ n^{2/3} and the volume ~ n^{4/3}, so perimeter ~ volume^{1/2} — the same B ~ sqrt(N) as a Euclidean disc. Metric balls in the UIPT also obey boundary ~ r^2, volume ~ r^4, again B ~ V^{1/2}. The sqrt(N) law is therefore *not* a Euclidean signature and a boundary exponent > 1/2 is *not* random-map-like; the Euclidean/UIPT discriminator is the ball-volume exponent (r^2 vs r^4), which the project already measures (r^2, core stays 2D).

### Cited Findings
- UIPT existence: Angel & Schramm, "Uniform infinite planar triangulations", Comm. Math. Phys. 241, 191-213 (2003), arXiv:math/0207153. — [arXiv:math/0207153](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0207153)
- Angel, "Growth and percolation on the uniform infinite planar triangulation", arXiv:math/0208123 (GAFA 13, 935-974, 2003 [journal not shown on the arXiv page]): UIPT ball volume grows as r^4 up to polylog factors; the boundary component of the ball of radius r separating it from infinity grows as r^2 up to polylog factors; site-percolation p_c = 1/2 with no percolation at p_c; the growth process is an efficient sampler. Mapping: analogous (contrast geometry). — [arXiv:math/0208123](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0208123)
- Curien & Le Gall, "Scaling limits for the peeling process on random maps", arXiv:1412.5509 [journal unverified]. Theorem 1: |dT_[nt]| / (p n^{2/3}) and |T_[nt]| / (v n^{4/3}) converge jointly to (S^+_t, Z_t), S^+ a 3/2-stable process conditioned to stay nonnegative, constants p = (2/3)^{2/3}, v = (2/3)^{7/3} for the UIPT. ### Inferences
- Derived from Curien-Le Gall Thm 1: P_n ~ n^{2/3}, V_n ~ n^{4/3} => P ~ V^{1/2}. From Angel / Thm 2: dB_r ~ r^2, B_r ~ r^4 => dB ~ B^{1/2}. In both the Euclidean disc and the UIPT, boundary ~ sqrt(volume). The sheet's compact-phase B ~ sqrt(N) is thus consistent with both; the project's r^2 ball growth is what rules out UIPT-like geometry. A post-N* boundary exponent above 1 is not reachable by any of these random-map geometries and must be a transient (B <= N trivially), which supports the "nucleation event" reading over "crossover to a new universality class".
- The UIPT has unbounded degree with a heavy-tailed degree distribution; the degree cap c=6 excludes it outright (see KQ3: all interior degrees <= 6 force nonnegative curvature, so no hyperbolic or 4-dimensional regime is available to the sheet).
- Peeling is a *local exploration* rule on a map already sampled from an equilibrium measure; the sheet is a *growth* rule with no equilibrium measure. The relevant analogue is Curien-Le Gall Thm 4 (Eden on the dual), which says Eden growth sees the same hull scaling as metric balls — Eden-type growth does not change the geometry's exponents, it inherits them. By analogy, the sheet's exponents should be read as properties of the graph it builds, not of the growth clock.

### Gaps
- No peeling result for bounded-degree triangulations was found; "uniform triangulations with degree <= 6" is a degenerate class (essentially the triangular lattice), so none is expected.
- Budd's peeling lecture notes were not located this session.

## KQ3. Bounded-degree planar triangulations: degree <= 6 => flat/polynomial growth; degree >= 7 => negative curvature and exponential growth

### Takeaway
Combinatorial curvature of a vertex in a triangulation is kappa(v) = 1 - deg(v)/2 + deg(v)/3 = (6 - deg(v))/6: zero at degree 6, positive below, negative above. Positive curvature everywhere forces finiteness (DeVos-Mohar 2007, proving Higuchi's 2001 conjecture; <= 3444 vertices, later sharpened to 208). Strictly negative curvature bounds the Cheeger constant and exponential growth rate from below (Keller-Peyerimhoff; Baues-Peyerimhoff); even a *sparse* set of degree-7 vertices (one in every ball of radius R, with min degree 6) makes a triangulation non-amenable (Angel-Benjamini-Horesh). For a disc with all interior degrees >= 6, |V| <= floor((n+3)^2/12) with n boundary vertices, equality for the triangular lattice. The sheet's cap c=6 forbids interior negative curvature entirely; its only curvature defects are on the boundary (dead corners are reflex boundary vertices), which is why the core stays 2D while the perimeter misbehaves.

Z. 268, 871-886 (2011), arXiv:0903.4793. Theorem 1(a): if C := inf_v (-kappa(v)) > 0 then the (physical) Cheeger constant alpha(G) >= 2 C_{p,q} C; 1(b): if c := inf_v (-kappa(v)/|v|) > 0 then the geometric Cheeger constant >= 2 C_{p,q} c, with C_{p,q} = (1 + 2/(q-2))(1 + 2/((p-2)(q-2)-2)) for finite p,q (sharp on regular trees). Proposition 1.2: for nonpositively curved regular G_{p,q} with C = -kappa >= 0, exponential growth mu >= log(1 + 2qC/(q-1)). Theorem 4: if |v| <= p and no cut locus, mu(G) <= mu(T_p) = log(p-1). ### Inferences
- Derived (standard; not quoted from a source): for a triangulated disc with interior degrees d_i and boundary degrees b_j, Gauss-Bonnet with equilateral angles pi/3 reads sum_int (6 - d_i) + sum_bdry (4 - b_j) = 6. A boundary vertex of degree b has turning angle (pi/3)(4 - b): b = 2,3 are convex corners (+2, +1), b = 4 is straight, b = 5,6 are reflex (-1, -2). A **dead corner** (b = 6 with both boundary neighbours at b = 6) is a run of three reflex vertices contributing -6 to the boundary term; since the cap forces sum_int (6 - d_i) >= 0, every reflex run must be balanced by convex corners elsewhere on the boundary or by interior degree deficits (positive curvature). Filling the dead corner would require raising some degree to 7 (kappa = -1/6), which the cap forbids — so the defect is a frozen piece of negative *boundary* curvature that can only be buried by growing around it, never neutralised. This is the combinatorial reason the fjord deepens rather than heals.
- Because interior kappa >= 0 everywhere under c = 6, the Keller-Peyerimhoff and Angel-Benjamini-Horesh non-amenability results cannot fire: the sheet can never become hyperbolic or acquire exponential ball growth. The r^2 core is therefore guaranteed, and the perimeter exponent >1 is purely a boundary phenomenon (consistent with the project's observation).
- Theorem 2 of Angel-Benjamini-Horesh gives, by complementarity, a direct diagnostic: for a c = 6 disc with no interior deficit (all interior degrees exactly 6) the Euclidean disc saturates V = (n+3)^2/12, i.e. B ~ sqrt(12 V). Measuring E = B^2 / (12 N) over N gives a dimensionless "Euclideanity" that should sit near 1 in the compact phase and rise as fjords lengthen B at fixed N.
- A triangulation with *all* degrees exactly 6 is the (3,6) = G_{6,3} tessellation, i.e. the triangular lattice (flat, kappa = 0, quadratic growth); this is implicit in the Keller-Peyerimhoff and Baues-Peyerimhoff treatment of G_{6,3} as the flat regular tessellation but I did not find a theorem stated in those words [unverified as a quotable statement].

## KQ4. Step pinning by immobile impurities (Cabrera-Vermilyea), poisoning, quenched-disorder KPZ: does one frozen site make a deepening notch?

### Takeaway
Cabrera-Vermilyea (1958) is the canonical theory of a growth front stopped by immobile stoppers: a step cannot squeeze between stoppers closer than twice the Gibbs-Thomson critical radius, giving a "dead zone" of supersaturation whose width scales as 1/(stopper spacing) in the CV regime; the front bows between stoppers and wraps around them. That literature is about *arrays* of stoppers; a single stopper is simply enveloped. In quenched-KPZ with negative nonlinearity a localized pinning centre seeds self-organised facets and a first-order depinning transition; with positive nonlinearity depinning maps to directed percolation. Eden growth with impassable obstacles gives a KPZ -> quenched-EW crossover (alpha_loc ~ 0.9, alpha_glob ~ 1.15, beta ~ 0.78) and "the front can only progress by lateral growth into the pinned region". No source was found that studies a *single permanently frozen boundary site* on an otherwise freely growing compact cluster; that case appears open.

### Cited Findings

Lee-Thorp, Shtukenberg & Kohn, "Effect of step anisotropy on crystal growth inhibition by immobile impurity stoppers" (NYU; venue/year unverified, ~2017): semi-implicit front-tracking simulations with anisotropic surface energy and kinetics; exact average-velocity formulas for the fully faceted case; local step geometry varies strongly with anisotropy while the average velocity is "surprisingly insensitive" until the min/max surface-energy ratio falls below ~0.1; benchmarked against CV (1958) and Potapenko (1993). Array, not single impurity. Mapping: analogous. — [RU summary](https://vsc.science.ru.nl/1998/98-02.html)
- Lutsko et al., "Crystal growth cessation revisited: the physical basis of step pinning", Cryst. Growth Des. (2014): extensive simulations "clearly falsify the CV model", a more complex picture reducing to CV in limits, covering mobile impurities and negative growth. — [IIT Comillas preview](https://iit.comillas.edu/documentacion/revista/IIT-14-211R_preview/Crystal%20growth%20cessation%20revisited:%20the%20physical%20basis%20of%20step%20pinning.pdf)
- Ranganathan & Weeks, PRL 110, 055503 (2013): terrace-step-kink model extended to impure solutions where impurities act as barriers to step motion. — [APS accepted ms](https://link.aps.org/accepted/10.1103/PhysRevLett.110.055503)
- Pina & Novais generalise CV ("step pinning model") to solid-solution/aqueous systems; "crystal growth inhibition by non-incorporating impurities has been described and quantified since 1958 by the so-called step pinning model". — [docta.ucm.es](https://docta.ucm.es/bitstreams/e520643a-7cd7-4438-933f-48790fc751ea/download)
- Jeong, Kahng & Kim, "Facet formation in the negative quenched KPZ equation", PRE 59, 1570 (1999), arXiv:cond-mat/9807194: first-order pinning-depinning transition; pinned surfaces form a facet with characteristic slope s_c when substrate tilt m < s_c; F_c independent of m for m < s_c, continuous transition with F_c(m) increasing for m > s_c; explained by "a pinning mechanism involving a localized pinning center and the self-organized facet formation". Mapping: analogous (single localized pin -> discontinuous macroscopic change). — [arXiv:cond-mat/9807194](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9807194)
- Follow-up (arXiv:cond-mat/0109162, authors unverified): the lambda < 0 class at and below F_c is governed by facet formation (triangular mounds with parameter-set slopes) and valley formation; mapped to a 1D submonolayer polynuclear-growth (PNG) model via constant facet velocity and random pinning events. For lambda > 0 the depinning transition maps to directed percolation. — [arXiv:cond-mat/0109162](https://web3.arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0109162)
- Review of growing interfaces in quenched disordered media (arXiv:cond-mat/9907036; authors unverified): below the critical driving force the interface is pinned, above it moves with velocity ~ (reduced force)^theta. — [arXiv:cond-mat/9907036](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9907036); Barabási & Stanley, *Fractal Concepts in Surface Growth* (CUP 1995), chapter "Quenched noise". — [CUP](https://resolve.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/fractal-concepts-in-surface-growth/quenched-noise/7E5AF55373631E9EFBC9A322BAA8562A)
- Gralka & Hallatschek (eLife 2019, see KQ1): obstacles "locally slow or stop parts of the front while other parts advance"; "the front can only progress by lateral growth into the pinned region"; unpinned sections outgrow pinned ones producing branch-like protrusions; at rho_c colonies become fragmented percolation-like clusters with rough exteriors. Mapping: analogous (closest published description of fjord formation from a pinned front site). — [PMC6513619](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6513619/)
- Eden model with quenched dilution (sites permanently blocked with probability p) and quenched acceleration: a long disorder-dominated preasymptotic regime, scaling near threshold set by 2D percolation, local roughness stays near 1/2 while large-scale exponents vary broadly. Hendrick, Trique & Manoli, "Disorder crossover in urban-front growth", arXiv:2604.21437 (April 2026). Mapping: analogous. — [arXiv:2604.21437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21437)
- Eden model with "acceleration sites" (Miyazima et al., Physica A 259, 1-9, 1998): critical concentration ~0.592 = square-lattice site-percolation threshold. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/phsmap/v259y1998i1p1-9.html)

### Inferences
- CV maps to the sheet as an analogy of *mechanism* (a stopper the front cannot pass) and fails on *geometry*: in CV the stopper is a point in the medium that the step eventually envelops, so no permanent notch forms; in the sheet the stopper is part of the front itself and stays on the boundary forever, so the notch cannot heal. The CV quantity that does transfer is the dead-zone idea: a region of parameter space (here beta) where growth near the defect is impossible, with a threshold set by a local "critical radius". The analogue of the Gibbs-Thomson critical radius is the minimum number of boundary nodes around the dead corner that must all be capped for permanence; the project's three-node definition is that radius.
- Gralka-Hallatschek's "lateral growth into the pinned region" is the closest published description of fjord formation from a pinned site: the fjord walls grow by the adjacent free front advancing while the pinned site stays; fjord depth then grows like the free front's advance ~ R, matching the project's fjord depth ~0.75 R. Their QEW exponents (alpha_loc ~ 0.9) are the best available quantitative target for the rough phase's -2.8..-3.1 spectral slopes.
- The dead corner is a *deterministic* permanent pin created by the growth itself, not a quenched-random obstacle field; the depinning literature is only analogous. The Jeong-Kahng-Kim picture (a single localized pinning centre seeds facets and a *first-order* transition) is the nearest analogue to the project's "jump within one octave" and is the citation for why a single defect can produce a discontinuous macroscopic change.

### Gaps
- No paper found on a single permanently frozen site on a compact Eden-type front; the morphology (fjord depth vs R, opening angle, wall roughness) appears not to be in the literature in this form.
- CV 1958 original (in "Growth and Perfection of Crystals", Wiley) not fetched; the Gibbs-Thomson critical-radius formula is not quoted because I could not verify its exact form from a source this session.
- Lee-Thorp et al. venue/year, and the author lists of cond-mat/0109162 and cond-mat/9907036, are unverified.

## KQ5. DLA fjords, screening, frozen interiors; fjord-depth statistics; Eden with a frozen site

### Takeaway
DLA fjords are characterised by the loop-size (neck-to-depth ratio) distribution, which converges to a self-similar limit with a finite minimum ratio (Barabási & Vicsek 1990), and by the harmonic-measure multifractal spectrum, whose alpha_max is finite because the deepest fjords are screened (Jensen et al. 2001/02, D_q infinite for q < q* = -0.17 +/- 0.02). Eden clusters have no screening (all perimeter sites equally active), so plain Eden has no fjords; the only fjord-like Eden morphologies in the literature arise from quenched obstacles, where lateral growth around pinned regions produces branches/channels. No study of Eden with a single frozen perimeter site was found.

### Cited Findings
- Barabási & Vicsek, "Self-similarity of the loop structure of diffusion-limited aggregates", J. Phys. A 23, L729 (1990): fjord structure described by n_R(x), the normalised number of loops with neck-to-depth ratio x inside radius R; for large off-lattice aggregates n_R(x) converges quickly to a limiting distribution with a well-defined x_min > 0 — self-similar loops, no long tube-like fjords, hence no phase transition in the growth-probability multifractal spectrum for typical clusters (caveat: rare anomalously thin loops could change this). Mapping: direct as a *descriptor* to borrow; the physics is different. — [CEU record](https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/self-similarity-of-the-loop-structure-of-diffusion-limited-aggreg/); [PDF](https://barabasi.com/media/pub_imports/files/4.pdf)
- A 1994 PRE on "lagoons" in DLA: lagoon-size distribution consistent with self-similarity but a surprisingly small most-probable neck width even for large lagoons [authors unverified; from a search summary]. — [CEU record](https://research.ceu.edu/en/publications/self-similarity-of-the-loop-structure-of-diffusion-limited-aggreg/)
- Jensen, Levermann, Mathiesen, Davidovitch & Procaccia, "Thermodynamic formalism of the harmonic measure of DLA: phase transition and converged f(alpha)", arXiv:cond-mat/0107024 (page says "accepted for PRL"; PRL 2002 [unverified]): iterated conformal maps resolve growth probabilities down to 10^-70; D_q infinite for q < q* = -0.17 +/- 0.02, hence alpha_max finite; large branches screen deep fjords more and more as the cluster grows. Mapping: analogous (contrast: screening-driven fjords). — [arXiv:cond-mat/0107024](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0107024)
- Mandelbrot, "The potential distribution around growing fractal clusters" (Nature 348, 143, 1990 [unverified]): points of highest and lowest growth probability can be close together; fjords have a "mainstream" with almost parallel equipotential walls; fjord ends are screened and inactive. — [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/348143a0)
- Screening breakdown: replacing unit random-walk steps with fixed-length flights weakens screening; clusters scale as Eden for N << N_x and as DLA for N >> N_x (crossover size N_x set by the flight length). Mapping: analogous (an Eden -> non-Eden crossover at a size N_x, but driven by screening, not by a frozen defect). — [arXiv:cond-mat/0403527](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0403527); scaling and crossovers in DLA — [arXiv:cond-mat/9909040](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9909040)
- Eden clusters are compact with fractal dimension 2, growing by choosing perimeter sites at random. — [R-universe DLA notes](https://johngavin.r-universe.dev/randomwalk/doc/dla_theory.html)
- Eden with obstacles produces branch-like protrusions and, at rho_c, fragmented percolation-like clusters (Gralka & Hallatschek 2019). — [PMC6513619](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6513619/)
- Mandelbrot, Kol & Aharony, "Angular gaps in radial DLA", PRL 88, 055501 (2002) — [not located this session; title/venue from memory, unverified].

### Inferences
- The sheet's fjords are *not* DLA fjords: there is no Laplacian field and no screening, so fjord walls remain fully active and the fjord should keep a finite opening angle rather than become a thin screened tube. The Barabási-Vicsek neck-to-depth distribution n(x) is nonetheless the right descriptor to borrow: for a defect-pinned Eden-like front the prediction is a *bimodal* n(x) — a background of shallow KPZ-roughness loops plus a discrete population of deep fjords, one per dead corner, with depth ~ R and neck set by the free-front advance since nucleation.
- DLA's "frozen interior" (growth probability exponentially small deep inside) is the opposite of the sheet, whose interior is frozen by construction (closed triangles) while the whole boundary is active except at dead corners. The nearest conceptual match for "frozen" in the sheet is Eden, not DLA.

### Gaps
- No fjord-depth distribution for Eden-type growth with frozen sites exists in the sources found.
- The 1994 "lagoons" PRE and the 2002 Mandelbrot-Kol-Aharony PRL are unverified.

## KQ6. Exponential first-defect size (Weibull shape ~1): nucleation language, Poisson first passage, Arrhenius dependence on a bias

### Takeaway
A constant per-event hazard gives survival P(no defect by N) = exp(-lambda N), a Weibull of shape 1; this is the standard "Poisson nucleation / first nucleation time" language, where the rate per site is an attempt frequency times exp(-barrier) (Arrhenius). The project's N* ~ exp(3.3 + 5.4 beta) is exactly a per-added-node hazard lambda(beta) = exp(-3.3 - 5.4 beta), with beta playing the role of (barrier x inverse temperature). Known deviations are diagnostic: short-time suppression of the first-nucleation probability (van Beijeren) and non-self-averaging rates when the barrier varies site to site (Sear).

### Cited Findings
- Nucleation as a Poisson process: if each potential nucleus forms independently, the probability of no nucleation in time t is exp(-J t), the survival probability of the metastable phase, so the first-nucleation time is exponentially distributed. — [arXiv:2601.12573 (cryopreservation review; authors unverified)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.12573)
- Per-site rate R_i = nu_i exp(-Delta F*_i), attempt frequency treated as constant across sites; when impurity-nucleus interactions make the barrier a random variable the total rate has the form of the Random Energy Model partition function and becomes non-self-averaging (realisation-to-realisation variation). Sear, "A statistical theory of nucleation in the presence of uncharacterised impurities", PRE 70, 021605 (2004), arXiv:cond-mat/0406019. Mapping: direct (language); analogous (heterogeneity test). — [arXiv:cond-mat/0406019](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0406019)
- Short-time corrections: if all nuclei start at the bottom of the well, the mean first-nucleation time exceeds the inverse nucleation frequency; for N nuclei the mean first time scales as 1/log N at short times versus 1/N for the long-time frequency. van Beijeren, "The uphill turtle race: on short time nucleation probabilities", arXiv:cond-mat/0205357 (submitted to J. Stat. Phys.; publication unverified). Mapping: analogous. — [arXiv:cond-mat/0205357](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0205357)
- Weibull survival S(t) = exp(-(t/tau)^k); k = 1 is exponential, k < 1 stretched, k > 1 compressed; used for delayed nucleation/fracture statistics. — [arXiv:1608.06357](https://arxiv.org/abs/1608.06357); standardised form S(t) = exp(-t^k) and the remark that deviation from exponential means the rate is not well defined — [MDPI Molecules 27, 3007 (2022)](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/molecules/molecules-27-03007/article_deploy/molecules-27-03007.pdf)
- Homogeneous nucleation rates are generally taken as Arrhenius exponentials in the barrier. — [arXiv:2504.00250](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.00250)
- A 2D Ising nucleation study found the distribution of nucleation times exponential at long times (constant rate) but suppressed at early times [source not identified beyond the search summary; unverified].

### Inferences
- The *measure* in which the hazard is constant matters. If a dead corner can be created only by an attachment event and each event has fixed probability q(beta), the hazard per added node is constant -> exponential in N (shape 1), as observed. If instead the hazard were per boundary site per unit time with B ~ sqrt(N) sites, one would get P(N* > N) = exp(-c N^{3/2}), Weibull shape 1.5 in N. The observed shape ~1 therefore says the defect is created *by the growth event itself* (a specific local configuration produced by one attachment+closure sequence), not by random boundary ageing. This is sharp and falsifiable; the Weibull shape fit is the test.
- With lambda(beta) = exp(-3.3 - 5.4 beta): if a dead corner needs m consecutive "unfavourable" moves each of probability ~ exp(-beta), one expects lambda ~ A exp(-m beta); the fitted slope 5.4 then suggests m ~ 5-6 such moves (or fewer moves with larger per-move cost). Speculative; testable by enumerating the minimal move sequence that produces a dead corner from a flat boundary.
- Sear's non-self-averaging warning transfers: if some seeds have an early "bad" boundary configuration (a locally high rate), the pooled N* distribution would show excess weight at small N relative to exponential. The two-decade spread at fixed beta is consistent with a pure exponential (the 99th/1st percentile ratio of an exponential is ~460, i.e. ~2.7 decades), so no heterogeneity is needed to explain the spread.
- Recommended language: "Poisson nucleation of a rare local boundary configuration with Arrhenius-like rate lambda(beta) = exp(-a - b beta); N* is the first-passage size; Weibull shape 1 = constant hazard per added node."

### Gaps
- No source combines per-site Arrhenius hazard + Weibull shape 1 into a single named model; the pieces are standard but assembled here.
- The Ising-nucleation early-time suppression result could not be attributed.

## KQ7. Random sequential adsorption / jamming: permanently blocked sites and jamming limits

### Takeaway
RSA is the canonical process in which sites become permanently blocked and the system reaches a jammed state; it supplies language ("jamming limit", approach to jamming theta(inf) - theta(t) ~ t^-1/2 for disks, t^-1/3 for anisotropic objects) but all standard results are on a fixed substrate. RSA on a growing/expanding domain was not found, so the mapping to the sheet (where the substrate is the growing boundary itself) is analogous at best.

### Cited Findings
- Review: Talbot, Tarjus, Van Tassel & Viot, "From car parking to protein adsorption: an overview of sequential adsorption processes" (Colloids Surf. A 2000 [venue unverified]), arXiv:cond-mat/9906428: adsorbed objects stay fixed (irreversible); adsorption probability falls because of exclusion until no non-overlapping placement remains — the jamming limit; for disks the saturation coverage is ~0.547 and near jamming the free surface breaks into isolated holes each fitting one more disk; theta(t) ~ theta(inf) - c t^-1/2 for disks. — [arXiv:cond-mat/9906428](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9906428)
- For anisotropic particles on a continuum surface theta(inf) - theta(t) ~ t^-1/3 for all shapes with nonzero proper area. — [arXiv:cond-mat/9402066](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9402066)
- Irreversible multilayer adsorption: higher layers grow increasingly via uncorrelated "towers" (deposition onto successive layers, not an expanding substrate). — [arXiv:cond-mat/9302033](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/9302033)
- Large deviations in 1D RSA (statistics of jammed configurations). — [arXiv:2009.08609](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2009.08609)

### Inferences
- The sheet is not RSA: the boundary grows while dead corners accumulate. The natural RSA-like observable is the *dead-corner density on the boundary*, rho_dead(N) = (# dead corners)/B. If dead corners nucleate at constant rate per added node (KQ6) and B ~ sqrt(N) in the compact phase, rho_dead ~ lambda N / sqrt(N) = lambda sqrt(N) grows without bound — so the compact phase must end; after the transition B grows faster and rho_dead may saturate at a jamming-like value. Whether rho_dead saturates is a concrete question RSA language helps pose (new path 3).

### Gaps
- No RSA-on-growing-domain or RSA-on-a-moving-front literature was found with the queries used.

## KQ8. Graph/lattice growth processes where growth at a boundary site requires a local condition whose failure is permanent

### Takeaway
I found no paper whose model matches "attachment at a boundary site requires a local closure condition, and failure of that condition is permanent". The nearest relatives are (a) Eden/FPP (local attachment, never fails), (b) Eden with quenched impassable obstacles (failure is permanent but sits in the medium, not created by the growth), (c) uniform-map growth schemes (local insertion, never fails, unbounded degree), (d) bounded-degree vertex-splitting tree growth (degree cap, but trees). The sheet appears to be a new model class: a degree-capped, closure-driven triangulation growth whose own history creates permanent frozen boundary defects.

### Cited Findings
- Eden with permanently blocked sites (quenched dilution) and acceleration: disorder-dominated preasymptotic regime, percolation-set scaling near threshold. — [arXiv:2604.21437](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21437)
- Eden with impassable obstacles: pinned sections, lateral growth, QEW exponents. — [PMC6513619](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6513619/)
- Face-insertion growth of uniform triangulations (local, never fails, no degree bound). — [arXiv:2110.14575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14575)
- Degree-capped vertex-splitting growth (trees). — [arXiv:0811.3183](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.3183)
- Static constraint results for degree <= 6 / >= 7 triangulations (KQ3). — [ar5iv 1604.05863](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1604.05863)

### Inferences
- The combination "local growth rule + hard degree cap at the flat value 6" is what makes failure permanent: a cap at 7 would let the rule bury a dead corner by creating one negative-curvature interior vertex (kappa = -1/6), which is exactly the Angel-Benjamini-Horesh Proposition 6 route to non-amenability if such vertices become dense. The sheet sits at the knife edge between "flat forever with frozen boundary defects" (cap 6) and "eventually hyperbolic" (cap >= 7 with closure failures allowed). This is a model-variant axis the literature supports measuring (new path 5).

### Gaps
- Absence of a matching model is itself the finding; it should be re-checked with terms like "kinetically constrained growth" and "irreversible self-assembly growth errors (tile assembly)", which were not searched this session.

## New paths (concrete, each grounded in a specific paper)

1. **Spectral vs local roughness, split by phase (Takeuchi 2012; López-Rodríguez-Cuerno 1997; Gralka-Hallatschek 2019).** Measure the local width w(l) of the radial excursion over boundary arcs of l nodes and the global width W(N), both about the *seed node* (not a centroid), after removing the k = 6, 12, 18 anisotropy harmonics. Compact phase: expect anomalous scaling (alpha_loc != alpha_s) — if alpha_loc ~ 0.5 with alpha_s ~ 1.5 the compact boundary is KPZ-rough locally but shape-dominated globally, reconciling the -4 slopes with Eden universality. Rough phase: compare alpha_loc to 0.5 (KPZ) and 0.9 (QEW at pinning) and the growth exponent beta to 1/3 vs 0.78. A result near QEW would make "defect-pinned front" quantitative.

2. **Weibull shape as a hazard-measure test (Sear 2004; van Beijeren 2002).** Fit P(N* > N) across seeds to exp(-(N/N_0)^k) at several beta. k = 1 => hazard per added node (defect created by the growth event); k ~ 1.5 => hazard per boundary site (defect created by boundary ageing, since B ~ N^{1/2}). Also test early-N suppression (van Beijeren) by comparing the empirical CDF to exponential for N < N_0/10, and non-self-averaging (Sear) by splitting seeds by early boundary-turning statistics.

3. **Boundary Gauss-Bonnet bookkeeping (Angel-Benjamini-Horesh; DeVos-Mohar 2007).** Log, per snapshot, the histogram of boundary degrees b_j (turning numbers 4 - b_j), the interior deficit sum_int (6 - d_i), and the identity sum_int(6-d_i) + sum_bdry(4-b_j) = 6 as a consistency check. Track (i) the count of reflex runs (>= 3 consecutive b = 6) = dead-corner count vs N (linear in N => constant Poisson rate per node; linear in B => boundary-site rate) and the density rho_dead = count/B (does it saturate, RSA-style?), and (ii) the Euclideanity ratio E = B^2 / (12 N) relative to the ABH Theorem 2 bound. The crossover should appear as E leaving ~1 at the first dead corner: a second, independent diagnostic of N*.

4. **Fjord loop statistics à la Barabási-Vicsek (1990).** Define loops as in n_R(x) (neck-to-depth ratio x) on the sheet's boundary and histogram x across seeds and N in the rough phase. Prediction for defect-pinned Eden-like growth: a bimodal n(x) — shallow KPZ loops at x ~ O(1) plus one deep loop per dead corner with depth ~ 0.75 R and neck growing like the free front; no self-similar continuum of x values (unlike DLA). This quantitatively separates "a few frozen pins" from "a screening instability", closing the already-withdrawn Mullins-Sekerka reading.

5. **Cap-7 variant to probe the curvature knife edge (Keller-Peyerimhoff 2011; ABH Prop. 6; Haslegrave-Panagiotis 2021).** Run `sheet` with c = 7 (or allow a single degree-7 interior vertex per dead corner as a "repair" move) and measure: dead-corner rate, the density of degree-7 vertices per ball of radius R, ball growth exponent, and the isoperimetric ratio |dS|/|S| on hulls. ABH Prop. 6 predicts non-amenability (exponential growth) once degree-7 vertices are dense at any fixed scale R; Keller-Peyerimhoff Prop. 1.2 gives the lower bound mu >= log(1 + 2qC/(q-1)) with q = 3, C = 1/42 for a uniformly degree-7 triangulation. This tells whether the sheet's 2D core is protected *only* by the cap.

6. **Pinned-site Eden control model (Gralka-Hallatschek 2019 as reference).** Run plain Eden on a triangular lattice with one perimeter site frozen at radius R_0 and measure fjord depth vs R, opening angle, wall roughness exponent, and the angular power spectrum. This isolates "what a single permanent pin does to a KPZ front" from the sheet's closure rule; the literature has no such single-pin study, so the result is a null model for the sheet's fjord-depth ratio 0.75 and bump ratio 1.22 and is of independent interest.
