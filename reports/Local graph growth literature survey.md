# Literature reframes the sheet crossover as pinned nucleation

Compiled 2026-10-16 from six research notes (one project-document inventory at HEAD 6a8ca11 and five literature surveys). Verification flags from the notes are carried through: **[unverified]** means recalled, not read from the source; **[abstract only]** means abstract checked, body not read; **[derived]** means a calculation from verified definitions, not a published claim. Unflagged citations were read from the linked page.

The literature contains no model that does what `sheet` does, but it supplies a complete vocabulary for what the project has measured, and it corrects four of the project's premises. The degree cap at 6 is the unique flat point of combinatorial curvature: an interior vertex of a triangulation has curvature (6 - deg)/6, so cap 6 pins the bulk to exactly zero, which is why the disc is 2D at all; Hua, Jost and Liu's theorem that nonnegative curvature forces at most quadratic ball growth means the exponential regime after N* cannot live in the degree-6 bulk and must be carried by fjord-lining boundary vertices **[abstract only]**. The dead corner is a boundary vertex of curvature -1/3 that Gauss-Bonnet forbids the rule from ever neutralising **[derived]**; its closest published analogues are Cabrera-Vermilyea step pinning (an array of stoppers, never a single one), Gralka and Hallatschek's Eden front with impassable obstacles (lateral growth around a pinned site, quenched-Edwards-Wilkinson exponents), and the minimal-neck baby universe of 2D dynamical triangulations that causal triangulations forbid by fiat. The exponential N*(beta) law is, in the language of Sear 2014 and Schulman-Winfree 2009, Poisson first passage with an Arrhenius hazard lambda(beta) = exp(-3.3 - 5.4 beta) per added node; the Weibull shape of 1 says the defect is created by the growth event itself rather than by boundary ageing (which would give shape 1.5), a sharp inference the project has not yet drawn **[derived]**. Four premises need correcting: B ~ sqrt(N) alone does not certify flatness (the UIPT also has boundary ~ sqrt(volume)); a single degree-7 vertex does not force exponential growth (a density does); "tree-like" is not "exponential" (critical trees have d_H = 2, d_s = 4/3); and fixed-window dimension slopes have no plateau on any small-world graph (Newman-Watts give the closed form). Three results appear novel: a parameter-driven Euclidean-to-hyperbolic growth crossover, a permanent closure-failure defect created by the growth itself, and the morphology of a single frozen site on a compact Eden front. Twenty-nine deduplicated new paths follow, nine of them re-analyses of the twelve saved 4M-node graphs.

## The sheet's nearest cousins all cap links or energy, never degree, and all come out hyperbolic

The closest published relative of `sheet` is the growing geometrical network of Wu, Menichetti, Rahmede and Bianconi (2015): start from a triangle, attach a new node to an unsaturated link to form a triangle, and with probability p close a triangle between nodes at distance 2. Its moves are the project's moves. The difference is the constraint: they cap the number of triangles per link at m, not the degree, so their bulk curvature R = 1 - k/6 is unbounded below as degrees grow, and the result is **exponential growth, infinite Hausdorff dimension and finite spectral dimension** ([Wu et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1412.3405)). Network Geometry with Flavor extends this to simplicial complexes and is hyperbolic for every flavour and dimension except the trivial chain ([Bianconi and Rahmede 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1607.05710v2)); the (s, d) table is **[unverified]**, taken from a search summary rather than the abstract page. The NGF manifold flavour s = -1, d = 2, where each link accepts at most one more triangle, is what `sheet` becomes when closure never fires between existing boundary nodes: a planar tree of triangles with infinite d_H. That is the project's beta = 0 limit, and the project's own finding that beta = 0 is tree-like matches. Random Apollonian networks, which insert a vertex into a face and join it to all three corners, are the uncapped version of the move set and have paths of length o(n), that is logarithmic diameter ([Ebrahimzadeh et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.5213)). **No degree-capped Apollonian or degree-capped growing-triangulation paper surfaced in any of the five surveys.**

The equilibrium relatives encode the project's two ingredients explicitly. Conrady's graph Hamiltonian has a vertex term c_v |deg(v) - 6| ("valence 6 corresponds to flatness"), rewards triangles and penalises an edge in more than two triangles; its ideal ground states are triangulated 2-surfaces, and at the lowest equilibrated temperature **8% of vertices are conical singularities** with a specific-heat peak that drifts to lower T with size ([Conrady 2011](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1009.3195)). Chen and Plotkin find low-energy states that are nearly 2D triangulations with handles and nonlocal links, a first-order transition, and a super-extensive-entropy argument that **T_c -> 0 as N -> infinity, so "infinite manifolds are always disordered"** ([Chen and Plotkin 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1210.3372)). This is the equilibrium version of the project's central result: a geometric regime that exists only up to a size set by the coupling. Quantum graphity argues rather than derives its lattice phase and never reports a ball-growth or spectral dimension; Conrady's spectral-dimension attempt explicitly failed at N_e <= 180 ([Konopka, Markopoulou and Smolin](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/0611197); [Conrady](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1009.3195)). The combinatorial-gravity programme of Trugenberger and Kelly reaches flat Z^D lattices from Ollivier curvature, but by Metropolis sampling at a temperature, on degree-regular graphs, with triangles forbidden so that the flatness motif is the square ([Kelly, Trugenberger and Biancalana 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1901.09870)). Their heuristic claim that defects are suppressed in the macroscopic limit is in direct tension with the project's finding that one un-closable defect dominates; the tension is real but the ensembles differ (equilibrium versus growth). Even their 2D geometric phase is not flat at large scales: it is a negatively curved surface with a curvature radius beyond which the spectral dimension rises to 3 ([Trugenberger 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.17526)), a finite-scale crossover where the project's N* is a finite-size one.

In statistical-physics terms the sheet is kinetic Monte Carlo growth of a triangulated disc with bond-counting energetics, and the Richardson/Cox-Durrett shape theorems guarantee a compact deterministic limit shape for any Eden-like process whose local attachment never fails ([Auffinger, Damron and Hanson](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.03262)). The compact phase is therefore the expected default. The one published sequential one-triangle-at-a-time growth that provably yields a d_H = 2 random surface is the layered growth of uniform infinite causal triangulations ([Sisko, Yambartsev and Zohren 2013](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.2869)), whose boundary length performs a diffusion; comparing that boundary process with the sheet's B(N), which sits at 3.75 to 3.85 sqrt(N) and then roughens, is a concrete borrow. Classical sequential growth of causal sets, the other well-developed local element-by-element growth theory, never produces manifoldlike sets: its limits are semi-orders with no spatial structure ([Surya 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1903.11544)), the same entropic obstruction Chen-Plotkin identify.

## Cap 6 is the unique flat point, and the theorems say the exponential regime lives on the fjord walls

Combinatorial curvature makes the degree cap exactly meaningful. For a locally tessellating planar graph, kappa(v) = 1 - |v|/2 + sum over faces of 1/|f| ([Keller and Peyerimhoff 2011](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0903.4793), full text read). With all faces triangles an interior vertex has **kappa = (6 - deg)/6: +1/6 at degree 5, 0 at degree 6, -1/6 at degree 7** **[derived]**. Three theorems bracket the cap. Positive curvature everywhere forces finiteness: DeVos and Mohar proved Higuchi's conjecture, with at most 3444 vertices for non-prism cases, later sharpened to 208 ([DeVos and Mohar 2007](https://www.ams.org/tran/2007-359-07/S0002-9947-07-04125-6/), abstract verified; Higuchi 2001 **[unverified]**). This is the project's c = 5 icosahedron at exactly 12 nodes in 5 of 5 seeds. Nonnegative curvature everywhere forces at most quadratic ball growth, |B_r| <= C r^2 ([Hua, Jost and Liu 2015](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.2826v2), **[abstract only]**, theorem restated through a secondary source). Uniformly negative curvature forces a positive Cheeger constant and exponential growth rate mu >= log(1 + 2qC/(q - 1)) for a face-regular tessellation ([Keller and Peyerimhoff](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/0903.4793)), and a triangulation with a degree-7 vertex in every ball of radius R is non-amenable ([Angel, Benjamini and Horesh](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1604.05863), Proposition 6). Wu et al. use the same curvature truncated to triangles, R_i = 1 - k_i/2 + t_i/3, and verify Gauss-Bonnet chi = sum R_i on their networks ([Wu et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1412.3405)); it applies verbatim to the sheet.

The consequence for the sheet is sharp. Because cap 6 forbids interior kappa < 0, the Keller-Peyerimhoff and Angel-Benjamini-Horesh non-amenability results cannot fire in the bulk, and Hua-Jost-Liu says a triangulated surface with all degrees <= 6 can never grow faster than r^2. **The exponential regime after N* therefore cannot be a property of the degree-6 sheet; it must be carried by vertices incident to large non-triangular faces, that is the fjords, which act as infinigons in Keller-Peyerimhoff's sense and carry kappa < 0.** This reconciles the project's observation that interior degree is exactly 6 in every run (fraction 1.000) with its observation that the 4M graphs lose their dimension. The structural prediction is testable by partitioning nodes into bulk, boundary and fjord-wall classes. Angel, Benjamini and Horesh also give a quantitative Euclidean reference: a disc triangulation with n boundary vertices and all interior degrees >= 6 has **V <= floor((n + 3)^2 / 12), with equality on the triangular lattice** (Theorem 2). Rearranged, a perfectly flat disc has B ~ sqrt(12 V), so the dimensionless ratio E = B^2 / (12 N) should sit near 1 in the compact phase and rise as fjords lengthen the boundary **[derived]**; the project's B / sqrt(N) = 3.85 gives E = 1.24, roughly the 15% perimeter excess over a perfect disc the project already reports.

## The dead corner is a frozen -1/3 curvature charge that Gauss-Bonnet will not let the rule erase

A boundary vertex of a triangulated disc with degree d has d - 1 triangles and, treating the outer face as an infinigon, **kappa = (4 - d)/6: +1/3 at degree 2, +1/6 at degree 3, 0 at degree 4, -1/6 at degree 5, -1/3 at degree 6** **[derived]**. In turning-angle language a degree-4 boundary vertex is straight and degrees 5 and 6 are reflex. The dead corner is a boundary vertex at the cap, kappa = -1/3, that cannot change, and the project's finding that the first dead corner is always an adjacent pair means the defect is born as a run of reflex vertices. Gauss-Bonnet for the disc with infinigon outer face gives sum kappa = 1, and with a flat interior the boundary carries all of it **[derived]**. A permanent -1/3 must be compensated by extra convex vertices elsewhere, which lengthens the boundary at fixed area; every further cap-saturated vertex the fjord wall creates adds another -1/3 to compensate, so B grows faster than sqrt(N) by about two extra degree-3 vertices per charge **[derived]**. Filling the dead corner would require a degree-7 vertex, kappa = -1/6, which the cap forbids; the defect can only be buried by growing around it, never neutralised. That is the combinatorial content of "the fjord deepens rather than heals". The caveat from the project-document inventory stands: the project asserts that the degree guard (condition iv of `_closable`) is the operative failure mode but has not run a census distinguishing the four failure modes, and the curvature reading above assumes the degree guard.

The analogues each match one feature. Cabrera-Vermilyea step pinning is the canonical theory of a growth front stopped by immobile stoppers: a step cannot squeeze between stoppers closer than twice the Gibbs-Thomson critical radius, giving a dead zone whose width scales inversely with stopper spacing ([Lee-Thorp, Shtukenberg and Kohn](https://math.nyu.edu/~kohn/papers/leethorp-shtukenberg-kohn-step-anisotropy.pdf), secondary; CV 1958 original not fetched). The CV literature treats arrays of stoppers in the medium, which the step eventually envelops; the sheet's stopper is part of the front and stays on it forever, so the sheet is the r_c -> infinity limit of CV. Kinetic Monte Carlo finds CV applies only to large impurities with long residency ([UB summary](https://www.ub.edu/portal/web/ciencies-terra/detall/-/detall/mineral-growth-close-to-equilibrium)), exactly the sheet's regime. Gralka and Hallatschek's Eden model with impassable obstacles is the closest published description of fjord formation: **"the front can only progress by lateral growth into the pinned region"**, unpinned sections outgrow pinned ones producing branch-like protrusions, and at the obstacle percolation threshold rho_c about 0.4 the interface switches from KPZ (alpha = 1/2, beta = 1/3) to quenched-Edwards-Wilkinson scaling with alpha_loc about 0.9, alpha_glob about 1.15, beta about 0.78 ([Gralka and Hallatschek 2019](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6513619/)). Fjord depth then grows like the free front's advance, matching the project's 0.75 R. In quenched KPZ with negative nonlinearity a single localized pinning centre seeds self-organised facets and a first-order depinning transition ([Jeong, Kahng and Kim 1999](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9807194)), the nearest analogue to the project's jump within one octave. Directed-percolation depinning (Tang-Leschhorn; Buldyrev et al.) is **[unverified]**.

The random-geometry analogues are the strongest conceptually. In 2D dynamical triangulations the unique local defect that destroys d_H = 2 is a minimal neck, a loop of three links pinching off a baby universe ([Ambjørn, Jain and Thorleifsson 1993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9303149), venue **[unverified]**), and the single constraint "no spatial topology change" is what separates the d_H = 2 causal ensemble from the d_H = 4 Liouville one ([Ambjørn and Loll 1998](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-th/9805108); [Durhuus, Jonsson and Wheater 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3643)). A fjord in `sheet` is a baby universe whose neck never closes. CDT's bifurcation phase is a published case where one singular vertex per slice, of exceptionally high coordination, changes the large-scale phase ([Loll 2019](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1905.08669)), the mirror image of a capped vertex that cannot grow. Self-assembly supplies the kinetic-trap framing: Grant, Jack and Whitelam argue that effective assembly needs both bond-making and bond-breaking, and the sheet has bond-making only, exactly the regime in which misbinding is permanent ([Grant, Jack and Whitelam 2011](https://arxiv.org/abs/1108.4542), abstract); the dead corner is a trap not describable by cluster size alone, Hagan, Elrad and Jack's second mechanism ([Hagan et al. 2011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1105.2830)). KTHNY disclination language does not apply literally, since cap 6 forbids interior 7s and interior 5s are transient; only the bookkeeping lesson transfers, that total disclination charge is fixed and a frozen negative charge forces positive charge elsewhere **[unverified]** for the KTHNY references.

## N*(beta) is Poisson first passage with an Arrhenius hazard, and shape 1 locates the nucleus at the growth tip

Sear's review of nucleation at constant supersaturation states the frame directly: for a constant nucleation rate the fraction of samples not yet nucleated decays exponentially in time, deviations are fitted with the Weibull distribution of extreme-value statistics, and in many experiments "the dynamic range of nucleation times is too large to be measured" because of variable impurities ([Sear 2014](https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2014/ce/c4ce00344f), abstract and summary verified). Replace time by nodes added and the project's survival curve P(N* > N) is Sear's curve. The project's **ln N* = 3.30 + 5.38 beta is a per-added-node hazard lambda(beta) = exp(-3.3 - 5.4 beta)**, Arrhenius in beta; the published model where a rare-event rate is exponential in a design-controlled barrier size is Schulman and Winfree's programmable nucleation barrier, where enlarging the tile set reduces spurious nucleation exponentially ([Schulman and Winfree 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0607317v2), abstract). Per-site rates of the form nu exp(-Delta F*) with a non-self-averaging total when the barrier varies site to site are Sear's 2004 impurity theory ([Sear 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0406019)). The project's "one crossover event per 3 to 4 M nodes grown at beta = 2" and its two-decade spread across twelve seeds need no heterogeneity: the 99th to 1st percentile ratio of an exponential is about 460, roughly 2.7 decades **[derived]**.

The measure in which the hazard is constant carries information the project has not extracted. If a dead corner is created by an attachment event with fixed probability q(beta), the hazard per added node is constant and the first-defect size is exponential, Weibull shape 1. If instead the hazard were per boundary site per unit time, with B ~ sqrt(N) sites, survival would be exp(-c N^{3/2}), **Weibull shape 1.5 in N** **[derived]**. The project's censored fits give shape 1.02 [0.49, 1.78], 0.91 or 0.80 depending on the treatment of seed 308, with likelihood-ratio p against shape 1 of 0.96, 0.75 and 0.45. **Shape 1 says the defect is made by the growth event itself, at a fixed-size active zone near the most recently attached nodes, not by random boundary ageing.** This is sharp and falsifiable, and it is consistent with the project's observation that the first dead corner is an adjacent pair born within about 1% of N_first. The Arrhenius slope has a natural reading too: if a dead corner requires m consecutive unfavourable moves each costing a factor exp(-beta), then lambda ~ exp(-m beta) and the fitted 5.4 suggests m of 5 to 6, or fewer moves with a larger per-move cost **[derived, speculative]**. The kinetic Tile Assembly Model gives the comparison: there the error rate scales as exp(-G_se) with exponent 1 per bond because one parameter sets both correct and erroneous bond lifetimes ([Winfree and Bekbolatov 2004](https://authors.library.caltech.edu/22768), abstract; kinetic-trap derivation **[unverified]**, Winfree 1998 Ch. 6), and proofreading tile sets raise the exponent by making the wrong path require several improbable events in series. The sheet's 5.4 is already a composed exponent. Known deviations are diagnostic: early-time suppression of first-nucleation probability when all nuclei start at the well bottom ([van Beijeren](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0205357)), and Sear's non-self-averaging if some seeds carry an early bad boundary configuration. The proper estimator for a constant hazard with right-censored runs is the exposure MLE lambda_hat = d / sum_i min(N*_i, N_cens,i) with a chi-square interval, and a Nelson-Aalen cumulative-hazard plot should be a straight line through the origin; this is textbook survival analysis, **[unverified]** against a named source here, not from the nucleation literature. Note the form is Arrhenius (barrier linear in beta), not classical nucleation theory (barrier proportional to 1/ln S or 1/(ln S)^2 **[unverified]**, Kashchiev), which places the sheet in the small-cluster or atomistic limit where the critical nucleus is a fixed motif of a few units.

## The edge spectrum brackets KPZ and pinned-front exponents, and the compact slopes are shape, not roughness

Radial Eden growth is compact with a lattice-anisotropic limit shape and 1+1 KPZ boundary fluctuations, alpha = 1/2, beta = 1/3, z = 3/2 ([Takeuchi 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1203.2483)). For a 1+1 interface the Family-Vicsek structure factor is **S(k) ~ k^{-(2 alpha + 1)}**, so a KPZ or Edwards-Wilkinson front gives k^{-2} ([arXiv:2303.10850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.10850)), while a super-rough Mullins-Herring front with alpha = 3/2 gives k^{-4} ([arXiv:0805.4160](https://arxiv.org/abs/0805.4160)); a spectral exponent that differs from the local one is anomalous scaling ([López, Rodríguez and Cuerno 1997](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9703024), venue **[unverified]**). Converting the project's slopes: the rough-phase -2.0 to -3.1 gives alpha_s = 0.5 to 1.05, bracketing free KPZ (k^{-2}) and the Gralka-Hallatschek pinned-front global exponent 1.15 (k^{-3.3}), consistent with a front that is KPZ-like away from the dead corner and pinned near it. The compact-phase -3.4 to -4.7 gives alpha_s = 1.2 to 1.85, super-rough. **For a closed curve a spectral slope steeper than -3 means the angular spectrum is dominated by the smooth large-scale shape, the deterministic limit shape and its harmonics, not by kinetic roughness** **[derived]**, so the compact-phase slopes say nothing about roughness until a local width w(l) over arcs is measured. The project's six-fold mode at k = 6 to 8 is the lattice-anisotropy limit shape of on-lattice Eden growth; on-lattice Eden "is ruled by the underlying lattice anisotropy" and even 2 to 2.5% velocity anisotropy "wreaks havoc" in width estimates ([Paiva and Ferreira](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0611159); [Southampton eprint](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/19148/1/Submitted_282_Jan_2006_29.pdf), authors **[unverified]**). Harmonics at k = 12 and 18 should be excluded before fitting, which is stricter than the project's post-hoc k >= 16 cut. Two methodological warnings transfer directly: Takeuchi found beta = 0.404 about the centre of mass but 0.333 about the fixed seed ([arXiv:0912.2717](https://arxiv.org/abs/0912.2717)), so the sheet's radial excursion must be measured in hop distance from the seed node, never from a centroid-like reference; and a k^{-2} slope cannot separate KPZ from EW, only the growth exponent (1/3 versus 1/4) can. The project withdrew its Mullins-Sekerka reading for the right reason: Eden and first-passage growth have no surface-tension instability and the shape theorem guarantees compactness, so fjords must come from pinning, not a bulk instability.

Peeling of random planar maps corrects an exponent bookkeeping the project might otherwise import. The "n^{2/3}" perimeter exponent is with respect to peeling steps, not volume: after n steps the perimeter is about n^{2/3} and the volume about n^{4/3}, so perimeter ~ volume^{1/2} ([Curien and Le Gall](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1412.5509), Theorem 1); metric balls in the UIPT obey boundary ~ r^2 and volume ~ r^4, again boundary ~ sqrt(volume) ([Angel 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0208123), venue **[unverified]**). A post-N* boundary exponent above 1 is reachable by none of these geometries and must be a transient (B <= N trivially), which supports the nucleation-event reading over a crossover to a new universality class. Curien and Le Gall also show Eden growth on the dual sees the same hull scaling as metric balls: Eden growth inherits the geometry's exponents rather than setting them, so the sheet's exponents should be read as properties of the graph it builds, not of the growth clock.

## Newman-Watts explain the estimator audit and the 20-shortcut threshold in closed form

The project's 2026-09-26 audit, which found that a radius-10 power-law fit on slowly exponential ball growth returns a confident window-dependent number, has its content fully present in three papers though none phrases it as a pitfall. Csányi and Szendrői define d from N_v(r) ~ r^d and alpha from N_v(r) ~ e^{alpha r} and state that **"if a finite, nonzero limit for d exists, then alpha = infinity; conversely, if alpha is finite then d = 0"**, adding that networks with mean distance under 10 offer "only two or three useful data points" ([Csányi and Szendrői 2004](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0406070)). Song, Havlin and Makse state that cluster growing gives d_f = infinity for small-world networks while box covering returns a finite d_B ([Song, Havlin and Makse 2005](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0503078)), so adding d_B would not confirm a dimension but measure a different exponent. Burioni and Cassi's "polynomial growth condition" is what the window audit tests; the Bethe lattice fails it, has infinite spectral dimension and exponentially decaying return probability ([Burioni and Cassi 2005](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/0507142)). Newman and Watts give the closed form: on a small-world graph the local effective dimension D(r) = r A(r)/V(r) is **1 for r << xi and 4r/xi for r >> xi, with no plateau** ([Newman and Watts 1999](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/cond-mat/9904419)). A fixed window reports where you looked. The project's window-stability audit is the operational implementation of the Newman-Watts D(r) curve and should be cited as such; it is also exactly the control that the Wolfram Physics Project's ball-volume dimension claims lack ([Wolfram Institute update](https://wolframinstitute.org/output/the-wolfram-physics-project-a-one-year-update); Gorard's derivation assumes rules preserve dimensionality, [Gorard 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.14810)).

The same formula predicts the portal result. Newman-Watts' crossover length is xi = (phi k d)^{-1/d}, which in the project's variables is **xi = (N/S)^{1/d}** for S shortcuts, the typical spacing between shortcut endpoints **[derived]**. The window audit should fail when the window's upper radius reaches xi, so the destabilising shortcut count is S* about N / r_max^2 in 2D; for N = 5000 and r_max of 15 to 20 that is 12 to 22, matching the project's k* = 20 on both planes. The prediction is testable by collapse against N / r_max^2, and the literature says destruction, not inflation: beyond xi the effective dimension grows linearly in r. Daqing et al.'s "dimension higher than the embedding space" is a different regime, a power-law link-length distribution giving a new power law, and in any case needs coordinates the project does not have ([arXiv:1308.6075](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1308.6075)). The d(p) fine structure, now read by the project as a smooth two-scale crossover, is what Csányi-Szendrői's "Euclidean at small scales, small-world at large scales" predicts. Barthélémy and Amaral's early finite-size fit of the crossover exponent (2/3 against the exact 1 in 1D) is a warning for fitting crossover exponents at N of 10^3 to 10^4 ([Barthélémy and Amaral 1999](https://amaral.northwestern.edu/publications/small-world-networks-evidence-for-a-crossover-picture)).

## Walkers, throats and spectra: effective resistance, integer cuts and Kesten-McKay

The walker contrast, 122 to 166x Pbar gain from an offset portal on `grown` against 1.0x quantum and 1.3 to 1.4x classical on the planes, decomposes into two known mechanisms, though no paper reports the contrast as one measurement. Classically, commute time equals 2m R_eff ([Chandra et al. 1996](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/BF01270385)); on a tree R_eff is the unique path length, so one shortcut collapses it by orders of magnitude, while on a 2D lattice R_eff grows only like (1/pi) log r and a shortcut changes little **[derived]**. Quantum-mechanically, continuous-time quantum walks on dendrimers stay in a small region near the initial node and transfer to other branches is very unlikely even at long times ([Mülken and Blumen 2011](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1101.2572)), which is the project's 30 to 100x deficit below equipartition on `grown` and its measured N x median IPR of 40 to 154 against 2.6 to 3.2 on the planes. The glued-trees exponential speedup is the wrong analogue: it rests on an exact column symmetry and dies under any disorder via Anderson localization ([Keating et al. 2007](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/quant-ph/0606205); [Jackson, Khoo and Strauch 2012](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1206.3178)). Mülken, Pernice and Blumen's small-world CTQW is the right one, and it makes a prediction that cuts against the project's equipartition reading: **random extra bonds on a ring make quantum transport fast but raise the long-time return probability above 1/N**, so the quantum walker on a shortcut-decorated 2D sheet should remain below equipartition even when fast ([Mülken, Pernice and Blumen 2007](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/0705.1608)). The project's Pbar x N of 0.97 to 0.98 on the planes should be checked against this. Mülken-Blumen also give a second route to d_s: the classical return envelope decays as t^{-d_s/2} and the quantum one as t^{-d_s}.

The throat threshold of about 2.6 strands on triangular, sheet and grown is most naturally an integer mixture. The project's observable, edge-disjoint strands threading a ball's boundary, is Menger's quantity for (interior, exterior), so per-sample thresholds must be integers. In the random graph process k-connectivity arrives at exactly the moment minimum degree reaches k, so thresholds are set by the weakest local spot ([Bollobás and Thomason via arXiv:2409.14398](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14398), exact reference **[unverified]**), and Coniglio's red bonds show one singly connected bond gates the whole current ([via cond-mat/0007129](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0007129)). With one strand the interior is a dangling cluster; with two it joins a cycle and the resistance drops from series to parallel. **A universal 2.6 is suspicious as a single number; a 60/40 mixture of 3 and 2 that is substrate-independent is the hypothesis**, testable from the existing per-draw thresholds. Why the mixing fraction would be capacity-independent is the genuinely unexplained part, and no literature was found on a transport threshold at a small fixed number of edge-disjoint paths.

For the Laplacian spectra of degree-capped tree-like graphs the reference law is Kesten-McKay: locally tree-like d-regular graphs have adjacency density d sqrt(4(d - 1) - x^2) / (2 pi (d^2 - x^2)), so the Laplacian has a **gap [d - 2 sqrt(d - 1), d + 2 sqrt(d - 1)] above zero**, the signature of infinite spectral dimension ([via arXiv:2609.05297](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05297); McKay 1981 details **[unverified]**; local law and bulk delocalization in [Bauerschmidt, Huang and Yau](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.09052v1)). A 2D sheet instead has flat Laplacian DOS down to lambda_2 ~ 1/N. Counting eigenvalues in (0, lambda_edge) per node is a window-free "has a dimension" flag, with the caveat from Burioni-Cassi that fitting rho(lambda) against lambda rather than lambda - lambda_min lets a square-root band edge fake d_s = 3 **[derived]**. The project's "Lorentzian spikes" have three candidate explanations, in order: delta-peaks from local motifs (two leaves on one parent give eigenvalue exactly 1) that Kühn's cavity method identifies as pure-point contributions ([Kühn 2008](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.2886)); Lifshitz-tail localized states near band edges, with discrete eigenvalues across the whole spectrum of sparse Laplacians ([Aspelmeier and Zippelius 2011](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1008.1087)); and genuinely Lorentzian Breit-Wigner resonances from a motif state hybridising with the continuum **[speculative]**. Sharp peaks in sparse locally regular graphs are the norm ([Farkas et al. 2001](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0102335)). No source describes Lorentzian line shapes in tree-like Laplacian DOS; the Cauchy connection in the literature is to Green's-function distributions on the Bethe lattice, **[unverified]** against Abou-Chacra, Anderson and Thouless 1973.

## Fixed-N rules: flows fragment, closure needs a loss term, bootstrap gives the droplet law

Every discrete Ricci flow in the literature is a weight dynamics that amplifies curvature contrast and, with surgery, cuts negatively curved inter-community edges. Ni et al.'s canonical implementation updates w <- (1 - kappa) d, "expands negatively curved edges and shrinks positively curved edges", cuts the top 5% by weight every 5 iterations, and proves convergence only for a toy two-block family ([Ni, Lin, Luo and Gao 2019](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.03993)). Convergence of the normalised Ollivier flow is proven for star graphs and, by later work, trees and girth >= 6 graphs; for general graphs with triangles it is open ([Bai et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2010.01802v9); [arXiv:2505.15395](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15395v1)). **The project's `ricci` is a flow of this kind and should be expected to fragment; its clumped fixed point and the audit's verdict are what the literature predicts.** Flatness from Ollivier curvature is reached only by equilibrium energy minimisation at a temperature with hard constraints (Kelly-Trugenberger), never by a deterministic zero-temperature local flow; no source runs such a flow and finds a lattice. SDRF, which adds an edge to support the most negative edge, is the one curvature rule that moves the other way, but nobody reports it producing lattice structure, and Bober et al. find the move set matters more than the curvature choice ([Bober et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2207.08026)). Sign bookkeeping matters before comparing: Ni et al. read weight as length, so "negative edges stretched then cut" equals the project's "negative edges removed" only if the project stores weight as strength.

Triadic closure alone on a fixed node set is monotone and terminates at the transitive closure, every component a clique, diameter 1; only the rate is empirical **[derived]**. Every published triadic model with a non-trivial stationary state adds a competing loss: node turnover ([Davidsen, Ebel and Bornholdt 2002](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0108302)), tie decay plus a friendship cap ([Jin, Girvan and Newman 2001](https://ideas.repec.org/p/wop/safiwp/01-06-032.html), venue **[unverified]**), or random link decay, where Marsili, Vega-Redondo and Slanina find **a first-order sparse-to-dense transition with hysteresis** ([Marsili et al. 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0307321)). The project's open question "what is the long-run state under `triadic`" is the lambda = 0 limit of that model, and its result that `triadic` on the sheet drives d_eff to 6.8 and then unstable is clique crumpling. Krioukov's theorem that strong clustering implies a latent metric proves only the 1D case ([Krioukov 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.01575)), so clustering alone cannot certify a sheet, and Bianconi et al. find triadic communities disappear at high density ([Bianconi et al. 2014](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.1664)).

The bootstrapping barrier has a rigorous cousin. Bootstrap percolation on Z^2 with threshold 2 has a metastability threshold **p log L -> pi^2/18**, so the critical system size is exponential in 1/p, a critical-droplet effect ([Holroyd 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0206132)); on G(n, p) the barrier is instead a power law, a_c = (1 - 1/r)((r - 1)! / (n p^r))^{1/(r - 1)} ([Janson, Łuczak, Turova and Vallier 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.3535)). Because bootstrap closure is monotone its final set is schedule-independent, so any schedule dependence of the project's barrier must come from `reinforcement` and `prune`, not from `activation` **[derived]**. Bornholdt and Röhl show that the anti-Hebbian half, weakening uncorrelated pairs, turns collapse-or-saturate bistability into self-organisation at the critical connectivity ([Bornholdt and Röhl 2003](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0109256)). For `majority`, the project's argmax tie-break toward state 0 is the "biased majority model" of Gärtner and Zehmakan, whose biased rule picks blue on every tie and, per a search snippet of the paper, reaches blue consensus from initial density near zero ([Gärtner and Zehmakan 2018](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10920), venue **[unverified]**, consensus claim from snippet); the tie-rule space is {keep, biased, flip} ([arXiv:2303.16536](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2303.16536)). Goles-Olivos says synchronous symmetric threshold networks have only fixed points or period-2 cycles while sequential update gives fixed points only ([via arXiv:1501.03992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.03992); original **[unverified]**); Gershenson finds transition locations robust to the scheme but attractor types not ([Gershenson 2004](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0402006v3)); Fatès shows synchrony rate can induce a directed-percolation transition ([Fatès 2008](https://arxiv.org/abs/nlin/0703044)). These together say the project's schedule-invariance result (P1 and the barrier) is the expected outcome and its underpowered residual is not a sign of a hidden effect.

## Four corrections to project premises

**B ~ sqrt(N) does not certify flatness.** The UIPT has ball boundary ~ r^2 and volume ~ r^4 ([Angel](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0208123)), and peeling gives perimeter ~ volume^{1/2} ([Curien and Le Gall](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1412.5509)); the Brownian map has d_H = 4 ([Le Gall](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1403.7943)). Boundary ~ sqrt(volume) is shared by the Euclidean disc and by a d_H = 4 random surface. The project's pre-registered compactness criterion (boundary exponent 0.5) is therefore necessary, not sufficient; the discriminator is the ball-volume exponent (r^2 versus r^4), which the project does measure, so the GEOMETRY verdicts stand on the window audit, not on B(N). The two-slope picture "B ~ sqrt(N) then ~ N" should be stated as "compact then ramified", not "Euclidean then random-map".

**A single degree-7 vertex does not force exponential growth.** Keller-Peyerimhoff's theorems need kappa <= -C at every vertex; a single -1/6 is a cone point with quadratic growth and a smaller constant; Angel-Benjamini-Horesh need a degree-7 vertex in every ball of radius R **[derived from the verified theorem statements]**. The docstring's "7 is negatively curved" is correct but the mechanism the dead-corner story needs is that the rule converts one permanent boundary defect into a growing density of cap-saturated boundary vertices along the fjord walls. That density, not the single charge, is what makes the structure tree-like at large scale, and it is measurable.

**"Tree-like" is not "exponential".** Uniform infinite causal triangulations are equivalent to the uniform infinite planar tree and have d_H = 2 almost surely ([Durhuus, Jonsson and Wheater 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/0908.3643)); generic random trees have d_H = 2 and d_s = 4/3 ([Durhuus, Jonsson and Wheater 2007](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0607020)). `grown`, with bases 1.30 to 1.60, is supercritical or Bethe-like, a different class with infinite d_H and d_s and a Laplacian gap. FINDINGS' phrase "tree-like at every scale" should read "supercritical tree-like"; a critical tree would pass the window audit.

**Fixed-window slopes have no plateau on small-world graphs.** Newman-Watts' D(r) -> 4r/xi means the pre-audit numbers for `grown` (2.2 at radius 10, 3.95 at radius 32) and the "dimension inflation" under portals were not approximations to anything; they were the window position. The audit's retractions are the literature's prediction, and the pruned-WS result that p > 0.2 is a small world again is Csányi-Szendrői's two-scale picture. A smaller correction: `grown`'s mean degree of about 4 regardless of cap is an identity (each attachment adds one node and two edges), not a generator puzzle.

## What is novel

Across five surveys and roughly 180 sources, no published growth model exhibits a parameter-driven Euclidean-to-hyperbolic (compact-to-ramified) crossover as a rate parameter varies: the complex-networks growth models are uniformly hyperbolic, the equilibrium models have finite-size or finite-scale geometric regimes but no growth, and the one tunable-dimension growth model is for trees ([David, Dukes, Jónsson and Stefánsson 2009](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.3183)). No model was found in which attachment at a boundary site requires a local closure condition whose failure is permanent and is created by the growth's own history; the neighbours are Eden with quenched obstacles (failure sits in the medium), uniform-map growth schemes (local, never fails, unbounded degree), and degree-capped vertex splitting (trees). No paper studies a single permanently frozen site on a compact Eden-type front, so the fjord-depth ratio 0.75 R, the bump ratio 1.22 R, the finger width 2N/B of about 370 and the wall roughness are unmeasured anywhere else. Three further items are new as inferences rather than data: the Weibull-shape-1 argument that the nucleus is at the growth tip; the Gauss-Bonnet argument that the post-N* exponential regime is a fjord-wall property; and the observation that the window-stability audit is a control the Wolfram-model dimension claims do not apply. Two items may be novel or may be artifacts: the 2.6-strand onset (an integer mixture would make it a mixing fraction) and the walker contrast (two known mechanisms, never measured together). The honest counterweight is that the degree cap at the flat curvature value is implicit in theorems from 2001 onward; the project's contribution is to have used it as a growth constraint and measured what happens.

## Conclusion

The literature changes the sheet's description from "a 2D disc that loses its dimension" to "a flat-curvature kinetic growth whose only available curvature defects are boundary charges, nucleated as a Poisson process at the growth tip with an Arrhenius rate, each one a permanent reflex run that Gauss-Bonnet forces the boundary to pay for with extra length until the fjord network, not the bulk, carries exponential growth". Every clause of that sentence is a measurement the project can make on graphs it already has: the curvature histogram, the Euclideanity ratio, the Nelson-Aalen line, the shell exponent, the neck census. The two cheapest of these (A1 and A2) would also settle the two live BRANCHES rows about the dead corner (44 and 45) more directly than a new growth scan, because they test the mechanism in curvature units rather than through the N* proxy whose estimator the project already knows lands 4 to 50% before N_first.

What the literature cannot supply is a precedent. The absence of a degree-capped growing triangulation anywhere in the complex-networks, random-maps, self-assembly or crystal-growth literature means the sheet's compact window, its single-defect crossover and its fjord morphology are the project's to characterise, and the right comparison objects are the ones built here: Eden with one frozen site as the null for the fjord, critical trees as the null for the audit, and a uniform random triangulation as the null for the perimeter. The owner's preference for running every applicable method and comparing is well matched to the state of the field: three dimension estimators disagree on exactly these graphs, and the disagreements are the result.
